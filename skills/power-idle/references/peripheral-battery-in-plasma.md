# A peripheral's battery in Plasma's own list (ORDER 700, 2026-11-18)

Who reads what, which routes let a battery reach it, where a user-space device can and cannot enter, and every trap met on the way.
Check freshness first: written on 2026-11-18 from UPower 1.91.5 (Debian; master of the same day read), PowerDevil and plasma-workspace 6.7.4, the edition's kernel 7.3.4-stereo3d18, `sparky-stereo-headset` 0.2.0 and HeadsetControl 4.3.0.
The general [power-idle](../SKILL.md) skill and [sparky-stereo](../../sparky-stereo/SKILL.md) come first; the package side is in [lights-and-peripherals.md](../../edition-packaging/references/lights-and-peripherals.md) and [device-tool-in-kde.md](../../kde-desktop-defaults/references/device-tool-in-kde.md).

## Who reads what

| Reader | Source | What it does with a headset |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power & Battery applet (`applets/batterymonitor` of the powerdevil repository, `components/batterycontrol` of plasma-workspace) | UPower on the system bus (`EnumerateDevices`, `DeviceAdded`) | lists it with its icon, state and percentage; the applet's title turns from "Power Management" into "Power & Battery" when any battery is listed; its tray item stays passive (inside "Status and Notifications") unless the computer's own battery charges or discharges |
| Info Centre, Energy page (`kcm_energyinfo`) | UPower (Solid) | one block per device: charge state, current charge, model, serial number, history |
| PowerDevil (`daemon/powerdevilcore.cpp`, `emitBatteryChargePercentNotification`) | Solid's UPower back end | a peripheral is any battery that is not the computer's own; at the transition to `PeripheralBatteryLowLevel` (`powerdevilrc`, `[BatteryManagement]`, default 10) it sends one `lowperipheralbattery` notification with the kind's icon (`audio-headset-battery` for a headset); not while charging, not at 0 % or when absent |

A battery of UPower's kind `battery` is the computer's own (the applet's `Primary`): a headset must arrive with a kind of its own (headset 17, headphones, mouse, keyboard, gaming input ...), or it is counted as the laptop's battery.
There is no second list of ours in the applet and never will be: whatever is not in UPower is not in these three places.

## Routes measured (the edition's kernel in a KVM guest, 2026-11-18)

1. **The kernel's own driver gives a `power_supply`** (`type Battery`, `scope Device`, a parent USB device that also holds the headset's sound card).
   UPower's `up-device-supply.c` finds the sibling nodes of the same USB device and reads `SOUND_INITIALIZED` and `SOUND_FORM_FACTOR` (set by systemd's `78-sound-card.rules`) on the sound card: kind headset or headphones (UPower merge requests 182 and 190, hadess, 2023; `wireless_status` hides the entry when a dongle's headset is off).
   Measured with upstream's own fixture of a Logitech G935 (`src/linux/tests/usb-headset.device`, replayed with umockdev through Debian's `upowerd` under the real Plasma session): "G935 Gaming Headset, Discharging 3 %" in the applet, the same layout and icon as route 3.
   On the edition's kernel three drivers do it: `hid-corsair-void` (16 ids), `hid-steelseries-arctis` (17), `hid-logitech-hidpp` (1): **34 of the 119 ids HeadsetControl drives** (read from the module aliases against `share/peripherals/headsetcontrol.ids` of the hardware package). Nothing in the kernel's master (searched `torvalds/linux` commits for HyperX and headset battery, 2026-11-18) covers the other 85; the mailing lists could not be read (lore answers a bot challenge), so a posted patch is not known.
2. **A kernel driver per protocol** is the same route for more ids: a driver per vendor, written from HeadsetControl's protocol and proved on each device; the cost is a kernel series carried and a device each, so it is not the route for 85 ids.
3. **A user-space daemon republishes the charge through `/dev/uhid`** (root only): a virtual HID device with the Battery System usages (Absolute State Of Charge 0..100 and Charging); the kernel's `hid-input` makes the `power_supply` (`hid-NAME-battery-1`, scope Device, model name the device's name, charging and discharging from the Charging usage), and UPower treats it like route 1 once a sibling input node says what it is. This is what `sparky-stereo-headset-battery` does, and what UPower issue 353 (svesh87, 2026-08-10, open) describes for a Corsair HS80 MAX.
4. **A user-space provider interface in UPower**: none exists (checked: `up-device-supply.c`, `up-device-hid.c` is for a UPS on hiddev, `up-device-bluez.c`, `up-device-idevice.c`, `up-device-wup.c`; merge requests and issues searched for provider, uhid, peripheral, userspace, headset); it would be a UPower fork carried on every machine. Not built.
5. **BlueZ's `org.bluez.Battery1`** reaches UPower already for Bluetooth headsets (merge request 344 widens it to several batteries); it is not a route for a USB dongle.

Routes 1 and 3 give the person the same entry (checked side by side in the applet). The ordering that follows from the design (the kernel first, then UPower's way in from user space): the kernel's entry wins where it exists, and the service stands down for such a headset.

## Where a user-space device can and cannot enter

- It can enter as a virtual HID device (`/dev/uhid`, `UHID_CREATE2`) and nothing else: UPower has no D-Bus or file interface for a provider, and a fake `power_supply` cannot be written to sysfs from user space.
- `/dev/uhid` is `root:root 0600`: a user service cannot do it, and giving every logged-in user access would let anyone type as the keyboard of the computer. A hardened system service does it (the package's unit has no capability, no network, `DeviceAllow` for `/dev/uhid` and `char-hidraw` only, `SystemCallFilter=@system-service`).
- The device only exists while the headset says its charge; it is destroyed (closing the fd, or `UHID_DESTROY`) the moment it does not, so nothing shows a stale percentage.
- UPower learns the kind from an input node of the same device. A battery-only HID device has no input node (hid-input drops an unpopulated one), so the descriptor carries two Consumer volume keys that are never sent, and a udev rule gives that node (`ATTR{uniq}=="PREFIX:*"`) `SOUND_INITIALIZED=1` and `SOUND_FORM_FACTOR=headset`: it makes UPower read a headset, the workaround issue 353 describes. The change that would make this a property of the device (read `UPOWER_BATTERY_TYPE` for a `power_supply`, as issue 353 asks) is not in UPower; ours is a udev rule until it is.

## Traps (symptom, cause, fix, how found)

- **Symptom:** the entry is in `upower -d` as `battery`, not `headset`, and Plasma counts it as the computer's own battery. **Cause:** UPower guesses a `scope Device` battery's kind from the sibling input nodes' `ID_INPUT_*` and falls back to keyboard; a headset has none. **Fix:** the udev rule above. **Found by:** reading `up_device_supply_sibling_discovered_guess_type` and the printed kind before and after the rule.
- **Symptom:** the entry is a headset but its model reads "NAME Consumer Control". **Cause:** a descriptor with two top-level collections makes hid-input name each input node "NAME" plus a suffix per application (`hidinput_allocate`, `HID_QUIRK_INPUT_PER_APP` with more than one application), and UPower replaces the model with the input node's name when it learns the kind. **Fix:** one application collection (Consumer Control) holding both reports. **Found by:** the model line of `upower -d` with the two descriptors, then the source.
- **Symptom:** no input node and no `power_supply` at all. **Cause:** the descriptor's one application is Telephony "Headset" (page 0x0B, usage 5), which hid-input does not give an input device. **Fix:** Consumer Control (0x0C, 1). **Found by:** an empty `input/` folder under the uhid device.
- **Symptom:** every read of the capacity makes the daemon receive `UHID_GET_REPORT` again, the first read blocks for seconds, the percentage stays unknown. **Cause:** hid-input asks the device for the capacity until it has seen one input report, and a report sent before `UHID_OPEN` may be dropped. **Fix:** answer `UHID_GET_REPORT` with `UHID_GET_REPORT_REPLY` (id, err 0, size, data starting with the report id), and send a report on `UHID_START` and again on `UHID_OPEN`. **Found by:** a prototype in the guest printing every event.
- **Symptom:** `systemctl start` of a service wanted by a device stops it at once. **Cause:** `StopWhenUnneeded=yes` and nothing wants it. **Fix:** start it the way it is meant to start, through the device: `TAG+="systemd"`, `ENV{SYSTEMD_WANTS}+="NAME.service"` on the hidraw node; in the rig a uhid hidraw node with the dongle's numbers and a rule that matches its HID parent (`KERNELS=="0003:VID:PID.*"`, since `ATTRS{idVendor}` needs a USB parent a uhid device does not have, and `ATTRS{uniq}` is an attribute of the input node, not of hidraw).
- **Symptom:** the package installs and the service is started and stopped in the same second (`policy-rc.d returned 101`). **Cause:** `dh_installsystemd` starts a unit on install. **Fix:** `dh_installsystemd --no-enable --no-start UNIT` for a unit udev starts; an upgrade then takes effect at the next plug.
- **Symptom:** the applet's popup shows nothing but "Power Management", and the Info Centre says "No energy information available on this system". **Cause:** UPower lists no device; the headset tray entry of the package shows the charge from its own read and proves nothing about the list. **Fix:** assert on `upower -e` and on the applet's title, never on the tray entry.
- **Symptom:** a proof over ssh ends with the shell gone, or `pkill` matches nothing. **Cause:** `pkill -f NAME` matches the remote shell's own command line (it contains NAME), and `pkill NAME` cannot match a name longer than 15 characters. **Fix:** `pkill -x` with a short name, or a pidfile.
- **Symptom (the rig):** ssh to a QEMU user-network guest times out in the banner exchange, though QEMU accepts the connection. **Cause:** the guest has no address; its NIC is `ens3` whatever `net.ifnames=0` says. **Fix:** a one-shot unit that takes the first device that is not `lo` and gives it 10.0.2.15/24 with the gateway 10.0.2.2. Typing into the serial console is too fragile for a proof (an open pager eats the next command): log in by ssh and send each script on standard input.

## The proof, in short

A guest on the host's own kernel and initrd (the edition's, with its modules copied into the image), an overlay per run, the stand-in of the program for a dongle the kernel has no driver for and a uhid device with the dongle's numbers: [kernel-guest](../../stereo-proof-rig/scripts/kernel-guest/) (`mkroot.sh`, `boot.sh`, `vmrig.sh`, `vmroot.sh`) and the package's `tests/vm/battery-proof.sh`. Red is the staged package without the service (UPower lists nothing, the applet says "Power Management", the Info Centre has no energy information, and the package's own notice is the only one at 8 %); green is the new package (the applet lists the headset with its state, the Info Centre shows model and charge, PowerDevil's own notice at 8 %, the entry gone when the headset is off or unplugged, ten right clicks of the tray entry still 10 of 10).
Nothing touches the real `/sys` or a real device: the guest has its own kernel, so a virtual HID device made there is not on the station.
