# An editor's canvas per eye: GIMP 3.2 through GTK 3's stereo area

Written on 2026-11-26 (queue row 137 with row 148, the stereo GIMP's milestone 1).
The stereo area itself is [GTK 4 and GTK 3 and GtkGLArea](../../stereo-window-3d-area/references/gtk-glarea.md); the depth layers are below ([Depth layers](#depth-layers-milestone-2-queue-row-148)); the deep and HDR canvas (milestone 3) is [an editor's deep and HDR canvas](../../deep-colour/references/an-editors-deep-canvas.md); what is left is in [GTK pieces still to build](../../stereo-window-3d-area/references/gtk-glarea-plans.md).

## What the person sees

- An image with the top-level layers "Left eye" and "Right eye" (how the stereo pictures plug-in opens MPO, JPS and PNS files), or a side-by-side or top-and-bottom picture the person put into one of the plug-in's editing modes, is shown in 3D: each eye sees its own content, at the canvas's own size, and Stereo KWin does the one resize.
- A packed picture shows each eye's view in both halves (the left eye sees the left view in the left half and in the right half), so the canvas keeps the image's size and coordinates and every tool works where it is used: a stroke in either half edits that half, and the plug-in's "Both eyes alike" mode repeats it in the other.
- Guides, the selection, paths, the layer boundary and the tools' frames and handles are drawn over the picture at the screen, the same overlay in both eyes.
- View > Enable 3D (on by default, per window, not remembered) and View > Display Settings (`kcmshell6 kcm_kscreen`) are the program's 3D options; nothing else.
- Where the canvas cannot be shown per eye (a window drawn by the processor on Wayland, an X11 window on Mesa's zink, a desktop without the stereo declaration) the canvas shows the left eye with its guides and outlines, and the status bar says why, in the person's language; the exact reason is in the log. Turning Enable 3D off and on tries again.
- A 2D image, and any image with Enable 3D off, is drawn exactly as Debian's GIMP draws it (the proof compares every sample of the screen).

## The route, and why

GIMP 3.2's canvas is cairo: `gimp_display_shell_draw_image()` renders the image chunk by chunk through GEGL into an 8 bit render cache (`gimp_display_shell_render()`), and the canvas items (`GimpCanvasItem`) are drawn with cairo over it.
GTK 3's stereo area takes OpenGL views.
The chosen route: **each eye is rendered by GIMP's own render code into a render cache of its own, from that eye's sources, and the two pictures go up as textures into one stereo GtkGLArea that lies under the canvas items.**

- The eye's sources: an eye layer's own buffer (a `GeglBuffer` view with `shift-x`/`shift-y` for the layer's offset), or the projection seen through a shifted view for each half of a packed picture, each clipped to its half. `gimp_display_shell_render()` became `gimp_display_shell_render_eye()` with a source list; the screen's picture is the same code with the shell's pickable.
- The area is GimpCanvas's **underlay**: a child outside GimpOverlayBox's overlay list (`forall`, `remove`, `size_allocate`), never drawn by the box, drawn by the shell's draw handler with `gtk_container_propagate_draw()` between the backdrop and the canvas items. The area clears its rectangle (the hole) and the window drops it from its opaque region, so the items GIMP draws afterwards lie over the picture.
- Each eye's picture is the whole canvas: the ancestors' backgrounds (the canvas window has none of its own), the padding, the checkerboard and the image, the same function the 2D canvas uses (`gimp_display_shell_draw_picture()`).
- Only what the canvas redraws is painted again in each eye's picture and uploaded (`glTexSubImage2D` of the exposed rectangles); the whole texture is blitted into the area's framebuffer turned over (cairo's first row is the top, OpenGL's the bottom).
- On X11 the image window gets a 32-bit visual on a compositing screen, so that GTK places the stereo child below the window (`stereo->below`); otherwise the guides and outlines would lie under it.

Why this route rather than a memory route in GTK (`wl_shm` pictures): the stereo area already does the subsurface or the X11 child, the declaration, the hole, the failure and the depth and colour state, and needs no change; GIMP's tools and items stay untouched; the depth road stays open, since the area takes half float and float views and a colour state, and GEGL renders in float (milestone 3). The memory route remains the way for a 16-bit integer picture on drivers that export no 16-bit dma-buf, and for a software renderer on Wayland; it is GTK's work, planned with milestone 3.

## Recipe

1. Read `app/display/gimpdisplayshell-callbacks.c` (`gimp_display_shell_canvas_draw_image()`), `gimpdisplayshell-draw.c`, `gimpdisplayshell-render.c` and `gimpdisplayshell-scroll.c` (the cache is moved on scroll), and `app/widgets/gimpoverlaybox.c` (children are offscreen windows drawn by `gimp_overlay_child_draw()`).
2. The module `app/display/gimpdisplayshell-stereo.c`: the model (eye layers or a packed picture in a mode), the eye caches and pictures, the area, its render handler, the told failure; built only with `gtk_gl_area_set_stereo()` (meson `HAVE_GTK_GL_AREA_STEREO`, OpenGL through epoxy).
3. The menu: `view-stereo` (a toggle) and `view-stereo-display-settings` in `app/actions/view-actions.c`, their callbacks in `view-commands.c`, two items in `menus/image-menu.ui.in.in`; the words are the stereo layer's ("Enable 3D", "Display Settings…") in its nine translations.
4. Build: the edition's recipe `sparky-os/packages/gimp` (Debian's 3.2.8-1, the patches after Debian's series, `release/build-from-lc-dsc.sh` with the staged gtk+3.0 as siblings).

## Traps

- **The whole canvas black in both eyes, the guides drawn.** Cause: GIMP's welcome dialog covered the canvas in that run; with a fresh profile GIMP shows it whenever `config-version` is missing or older, whatever `show-welcome-dialog` says. Fix: `(config-version "3.2.8")` in the test profile's `gimprc`. Found in a reduced capture of the run.
- **Every `gimprc` value ignored.** Cause: one unknown key (`single-window-mode` is not a `gimprc` key) is a fatal parse error and GIMP uses defaults for the whole file (`Gimp-Config-Message ... fatal parse error` in GIMP's output). Fix: only `gimprc` keys; the window's size goes into `sessionrc` (`(session-info "toplevel" (factory-entry "gimp-single-image-window") (position 0 0) (size W H))`).
- **The padding black in both eyes, the image right.** Cause: the canvas's window has no background; in 2D what shows there is what the widgets around it painted underneath (the theme's 60, 60, 60). Fix: each eye's picture starts with the CSS backgrounds of the canvas's ancestors, top level first, each at its place relative to the canvas (`gtk_render_background()`). Found by dumping each eye's picture to PNG (alpha 0 outside the image).
- **Both eyes one image pixel off after scrolling at 400 %.** Cause: `gimp_display_shell_scroll()` moves the render cache's pixels and translates its valid region instead of rendering again; the eye caches stayed valid with the old content. Fix: the scroll moves each eye's cache the same way (GIMP's own technique). Found by comparing the guides (drawn by the canvas, at the right place) with the squares (one pixel off) in the same capture; a GEGL test showed a shifted buffer view reads exactly like its buffer.
- **The area drawn over the guides.** Cause: a container child is drawn after the shell's draw handler, which already drew the items. Fix: the area is an underlay that GimpOverlayBox's own draw never draws, and the shell draws it before the items. GimpOverlayBox's overlay children are offscreen windows: no place for an OpenGL area.
- **The left view shown without the guides where the pair cannot be shown.** Symptom: on llvmpipe under Wayland the status bar said why and the left eye showed, but the guides and the layer's dashed boundary were missing (695 samples in the image's place differed from the 2D control). Cause: GTK's area draws its fallback left view with OpenGL (`gdk_cairo_draw_from_gl`), which adds that rectangle to the paint's flushed region; at the end of the paint GTK 3 subtracts the flushed region from the cairo picture it uploads (`gdkwindow.c`, `gdk_window_end_paint_internal`), unless what was drawn over it was marked for blending with `gdk_window_mark_paint_from_clip()`, which nothing calls for items drawn later in the same draw handler. So the guides were dropped. Fix: once the area reports it cannot show the pair, GIMP hides it and draws the left eye itself with cairo (`gimp_display_shell_draw_picture()` with the left eye), the items after it; turning Enable 3D off and on tries the pair again. Found by putting the capture over its control.
- **The whole window black on X11 with zink, and still black with Enable 3D off.** Symptom: under Xwayland with Mesa's zink (the edition's route for OpenGL programs on an NVIDIA desktop), a stereo image's window was black everywhere. Cause: GTK 3's own fault, recorded by the GTK 3 lane (Debian's GTK 3 too): a window given an OpenGL context is drawn with OpenGL from then on, and with zink that shows black. Fix in GIMP: on X11 the renderer is read first on a throwaway 1 x 1 window (in GTK 3 a context turns only its own window to OpenGL painting); with zink the canvas's window is given no area, the canvas draws the left eye and the reason is told. GTK's own fix stays owed. Found because the flat row failed too: the fault was the window's, not the pair's.
- **GIMP's meson asks for `INSTALL.in`.** Cause: a tree holding `.git` is taken for a development checkout. Fix: keep the baseline repository beside the tree (`git init --separate-git-dir`, then remove the `.git` file).
- **`gen-languages: setlocale() failed`.** GIMP's build needs a UTF-8 locale; Debian's rules run it under `localehelper LANG=en_US.UTF-8`.
- **(packaging) deb-multimedia's libmypaint wins the build.** Its `libmypaint-dev` has epoch 1 (1:1.6.1), Debian's has none (1.6.0), so a build with both sources links `libmypaint.so.0` where Debian's GIMP links `libmypaint-1.5.so.1` (the only difference in `ldd` over the program and its plug-ins); a bound `libmypaint-dev (<< 1:0)` is refused by apt's solver ("Reached two conflicting assignments"). `release/build-from-lc-dsc.sh` adds deb-multimedia to every build, so the edition's GIMP is built that way and says so in its README.

## Depth layers (milestone 2, queue row 148)

Daniel's design (2026-10-15): "we'll add a special layer type that group other layers in one depth value the artist sets, with 0.000 precision ... the same 2D movement you do with the mouse + a handy key pressed at the same time make it "pop", other make it sink".
Built in gimp 3.2.8-1+stereo3d2 on stereo-layer 1.24.0.

### What the person sees

- Layer > New Depth Layer puts the selected layers into a depth layer at 0.000 (a layer group with a value).
- The Layers dock shows a Depth row (pixels of separation, three decimals, never clamped) for the depth layer the selected layer is in; the row is hidden otherwise, so a 2D document's window is as before.
- Holding 8 (pop) or 7 (sink) over the canvas turns the pointer's movement into depth, 0.1 pixel of separation for each pixel moved, with the tool's drag or without it; the tool does not see that movement; one undo step a gesture. From the Layer menu each moves the depth by one pixel. Both are ordinary actions (`layers-depth-pop`, `layers-depth-sink`), rebindable in Keyboard Shortcuts.
- Each eye sees every depth layer moved by half its value, the left eye to the left for a positive value (behind the screen); layers in no depth layer stay at the screen; a depth layer inside another is at its own value, never the sum.
- With the eye layers "Left eye" and "Right eye" too, each eye shows its own eye layer under the depth layers. A picture in a packed editing mode keeps its depth layers at the screen and the status bar says so.
- XCF keeps the value as the group's persistent parasite `gimp-depth-layer` ("12.000"): Debian's GIMP opens a plain layer group and loses nothing, and saving it there keeps the value.
- The stereo pictures plug-in (0.3.0) exports MPO, JPS and PNS of an image with depth layers from each eye's picture as the canvas shows it (`gimp-depth-layer-new-from-eye`).

### The model, shared

libstereo-policy's `stereo-depth.h` holds what GIMP and later Krita share: `stereo_depth_parse()` and `stereo_depth_format()` (three decimals in the C locale whatever the program's locale, by integer arithmetic), `stereo_depth_eye_shift()` (-s/2 left, +s/2 right), and `stereo_depth_shift_rgba()` (8, 16-bit or float RGBA; a whole move copies the samples, a fractional one resamples premultiplied samples with a Lanczos window of three lobes, the edition's choice: no recorded design names a kernel).

### Each eye's picture: the route

GIMP's image graph composites each layer over the layers below it (`GimpFilterStack`), and a normal group's content is its own projection buffer, rendered once for 2D. So an eye cannot be a property of the image's graph: everything above a moved layer differs between the eyes.
`GimpEyeProjectable` (one per eye) builds the eye's own chain: for each visible layer, bottom first, a copy of its mode node (same operation, every property copied) fed by the eye's picture below, while the layer's content and mask come from the image's graph as they are (the producers of the mode node's `aux` and `aux2`, fanned out), moved by `gimp:depth-shift` when the layer is at a depth. A group whose layers are all at one depth is moved as one picture (its projection, one resampling); a group holding layers at another depth, and every pass-through group, is composited again from its layers. So every layer mode, opacity, mask and effect is GIMP's own. The chain is made again when a walk of the layer tree describes a different structure (layers, visibility, mode properties, producers, depths), and every image change reaches the eye widened by the largest move plus the window's reach.
The display's stereo module shows an image with depth layers as a third kind: each eye's source is its `GimpProjection`, whose updates redraw the canvas.

## Krita (queue row 148, milestone 1): the canvas per eye on the Qt side

Built in krita 1:6.0.4.1+dfsg-1+stereo3d1+lc1 (Debian's 1:5.3.4.1+dfsg-1, which is upstream's krita-6.0.4.1 tree, built as Krita's Qt 6 flavour) on the edition's Qt 6.11.2 and the stereo layer 1.22.0.
The fork is `invent.kde.org/danielcamposramos/krita`, branch `order148b-canvas-per-eye` on upstream's tag v6.0.4.1; Krita's README sets a moratorium on AI-assisted contributions, so the work stays in the fork and the package.

### What the person sees

- A document whose file the stereo layer reads as a pair packed side by side or top and bottom (the file's own mark, such as PNG's `sTER` or JPS's descriptor, then the person's choice for that file, then the shape of an unmarked picture), or a document with the top-level layers "Left eye" and "Right eye", is shown in 3D: each eye sees its own picture at the canvas's size, and Stereo KWin does the one resize.
- A packed picture shows each eye's view in both halves (GIMP's model), so the image keeps its size and coordinates and every tool works where it is used; the earlier "Paint on the Right View" and its one-half view are gone with it.
- Guides, the selection, the brush outline and every other decoration are drawn over the picture by the window, at the screen, the same in both eyes.
- View > Enable 3D (the stereo layer's own action and words, on in every new window, for the window and the session), View > 3D Input Format (the layer's menu of every packing a still can hold, for the document's file, kept in the shared per-file store) and View > Display Settings (`kcmshell6 kcm_kscreen`) are the program's 3D options. Krita already binds Alt+Shift+N, the photo programs' Enable 3D key, to "Select Normal Blending Mode", so Enable 3D has no default key in Krita.
- Where the pair cannot be shown the canvas shows the left eye and says why (on the canvas, in the log, in Enable 3D's tooltip): Krita on X11, OpenGL ES, a stereo surface the graphics stack refuses, NVIDIA's own OpenGL (which does not show the layer; the edition's session on an NVIDIA desktop uses zink, which does). In wrap-around mode and on the QPainter canvas it shows the picture as it is, and says that.
- A 2D document, and any document with Enable 3D off, is drawn as the published build draws it, sample for sample, the brush outline included (the inverse of the picture under it, so it shows on every colour); on Wayland it goes through the canvas layer too (one buffer), with its decorations and the outline painted on that layer's picture.

### The route, and why

Krita's canvas is a `QOpenGLWidget` (`KisOpenGLCanvas2`) that draws the image from texture tiles (`KisOpenGLImageTextures`) and then its decorations with `QPainter`.
The October work (+stereo3d3) already gave it a layer of its own under Qt's Wayland platform: `KisStereoCanvasLayer`, a child `QWindow` with left and right buffers in the canvas's own (deep) surface format, placed below the main window's surface with `wl_subsurface.place_below`; the renderer paints each eye into a texture of its own (`paintCanvasToEyeTexture()`), the layer blits them into its two buffers, the edition's Mesa packs them into one full side-by-side buffer and declares it, and the main window, translucent from its creation, leaves a hole over the canvas and draws the decorations there.
Row 148 kept that and changed what each eye's texture holds, and where a 2D canvas's decorations go:
- **Each eye has textures of its own:** two more `KisOpenGLImageTextures`, made as the image's own are (the display config, the colour management state, the proofing config, the channel flags) and fed through the texture set's own `updateInfoBuilder().buildUpdateInfo(rect, device, bounds, lod, convert)`: from the eye layer's projection, or, for a packed picture, from a paint device of the eye's own into which the eye's view is copied from the image's projection, into both halves (`KisPainter::copyAreaOptimized()`). So each eye's picture is exactly what a 2D document of that view shows, and the texture filter never reaches the other view. They are refreshed from every full-resolution update rectangle the canvas receives, while the image is at its full resolution; the image's own textures and the display shader are untouched, and `drawImage()` only chooses which texture set to draw.
- **What the document is:** `KisCanvas2::updateStereoSource()` looks for the eye layers, else asks the stereo layer, `Stereo::Picture::loadPair(path, QSize(64, 64), {})` (decoded to a small bound; empty for a file that is not a pair), which applies the file's mark, the person's choice and the shape in that order; `eyeOrder` says which half is the left eye, the person's swap included (`Picture::swapped()` flips it). It looks again when a layer is added, removed or renamed (a 200 ms compressor), the document's path changes or it is saved, its size changes, or the person's choice for the file changes (`FileChoiceMenu::changed`). A document resized away from its file's size is not read from the file until it is saved.
- **A layer with one buffer carries its decorations:** every canvas of a translucent window goes through a layer (a 2D one through a layer with one buffer, which keeps the deep surface), and a widget left transparent over it cannot draw Krita's brush outline, which inverts what is under it with a subtracting blend in the framebuffer it is drawn into. So for one buffer the canvas paints its decorations (`drawDecorations()`, the tool's outline included) with `QPainter` on a `QOpenGLPaintDevice` while the eye framebuffer is still bound (`paintCanvasToEyeTexture(eye, paintOver)`), and the widget draws nothing over it. Qt's GL2 paint engine binds no framebuffer of its own (a plain `QOpenGLPaintDevice::ensureActiveTarget()` is empty; the widget's device only rebinds its framebuffer and is not flipped), so the picture comes out as the widget's would; the eye framebuffer has a depth and stencil attachment, as the widget's has, because the engine fills paths and clips through the stencil. A stereo pair keeps its decorations on the widget, at the screen plane.

### Recipe

1. The fork branch carries the October series (native Wayland under a Wayland compositor, the 16-bit surface, the canvas layer, its colour description, the deep translucent window, the resource cache's single transaction), upstream's SIP commits (Dmitry Kazakov, `30182dbf`, `bf7fa438`) in place of ours, upstream's native Wayland toolbar fix (`cd944914`), and row 148's six commits.
2. ### The proof

One container per row: Stereo KWin on a 1920 x 1080 virtual output, Krita with a fresh profile, driven by a Python plugin of the rig (commands read from a file, each answered with the actions' states): open, maximize, zoom to fit, guides, rotation, Enable 3D on and off, Display Settings, a float document made in place, and the pointer (the rig's compositor has no mouse, so an enter and a move event are sent to the canvas widget, `KisOpenGLCanvas2`, through Krita's input manager, and the tool draws its outline there).

### Traps

Fix: each eye's own textures from a device that holds the eye's view in both halves, so the picture is a 2D picture of that view (the stencil and offset passes went).
