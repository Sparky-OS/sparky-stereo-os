# A Qt 6 OpenGL view as a declared 3D area, fitted to the person's limits (OpenSCAD)

**For** anyone giving a Qt 6 program's `QOpenGLWidget` 3D view a stereo pair on the edition, where the program draws one picture upstream and we add the two cameras: the route, how the view reads the person's depth limits, the fit on a CSG preview and on a rendered mesh, the options, the proofs and the traps.
Learned on OpenSCAD 2026.11-RC1 (queue row 50, step 4, November 2026), on Stereo KWin 6.7.4 +stereo3d64, Mesa 26.1.6 +stereo3d29, qt6-base 6.11.2 +stereo3d23 and libstereo-declare 1.12.2.

## Which case it is

Grep upstream's own tree, never our patch: OpenSCAD's RC1 asks for no stereo buffers (`QGLView`'s format, `src/gui/QGLView.cc`), draws once in `paintGL()` and has one eye in `GLView::setupCamera()`; its only stereo words are glad's generated GL defines.
So it is a scene where we add the cameras: fitted to the person's limits, no separation setting.

## The recipe

1. **The view's own format, never the default one.** Ask for `QSurfaceFormat::StereoBuffers` in the 3D view's own `setFormat()` when Enable 3D is on, and leave `QSurfaceFormat::setDefaultFormat()` alone: the edition's qt6-base gives a stereo `QOpenGLWidget` a native window of its own (on Wayland a subsurface that Mesa declares with `set_content(3)`, on xcb a child window Mesa lists), and the rest of the window stays one picture in both eyes. Our upstream offer (pull request 7112) set it on the default format, which on our Qt would make every GL window of the program ask for stereo.
2. **One eye per `paintGL()`.** Qt calls `paintGL()` for the left buffer, then the right (`currentTargetBuffer()`, Qt 6.5). Perspective: move the eye sideways along the camera's right vector and shift the frustum by `-offset * near / C` (both eyes meet at the distance `C` of the point looked at). Orthographic: keep the eye and shear the projection about the point looked at, `x' = x + s (d - C)` with `s = offset / C` (column-major `{1,0,0,0, 0,1,0,0, -s,0,1,0, -s*C,0,0,1}` after `glOrtho`); turning the eye is toe-in and is not used.
3. **The limits.** Read them through libstereo-declare's buffer call, in the view's own buffer pixels: Wayland `stereo_depth_wayland_get_buffer_limits()` with the **top-level window's** `wl_surface` (Qt's `QPlatformNativeInterface::nativeResourceForWindow("surface", window()->windowHandle())`, `nativeResourceForIntegration("wl_display")`; a subsurface gets no limits), X11 `stereo_depth_x11_get_buffer_limits()` on the view's own window (`winId()` of the stereo widget, which the edition's Qt makes native), buffer and shown width both the view's width times its device pixel ratio. Keep the library's header (it includes Xlib, which defines `Status`, `Bool`, `None`) in a translation unit of its own. Poll four times a second; a change redraws the open view.
4. **The fit, by pixels.** Before the left eye, draw the whole view once from the camera between the eyes into the left buffer, and in a hook after the scene and before the 2D items over it read the depth (`glReadPixels`, `GL_DEPTH_COMPONENT`, `GL_FLOAT`; the cleared depth is the background and is not counted). Convert the nearest and farthest window depth to distances with that pass's projection (perspective `D = B / ((2d - 1) + A)`, orthographic `D = (B - (2d - 1)) / A`, `A = P[10]`, `B = P[14]`), `k` = half the buffer's width times `P[0]`, and the separation `t` (a fraction of `C`) is the smallest of `popped / (k (C/Dnear - 1))` and `sunk / (k (1 - C/Dfar))` (orthographic: `popped / (k (C - Dnear))`, `sunk / (k (Dfar - C))`); with nothing off the plane of the point looked at, perspective puts infinity at the sunk limit. The left eye then draws over the measuring pass. The same pass serves OpenSCAD's preview (OpenCSG) and its rendered model (F6), because both draw through `GLView::paintGL()`.
5. **Picking from the camera between the eyes.** A double click reads the depth at the pixel: draw the view once from the camera between the eyes into the current buffer first, so the point found is the one a single view finds, then schedule a repaint for the eyes.
6. **Options.** Enable 3D, on by default and read when a window's view is made ("applies to new windows"), and a button that opens Display Settings (`systemsettings kcm_kscreen`, else `kcmshell6 kcm_kscreen`, and say so when neither starts). No separation, no swap.
7. **Tell, once, in the console the program already has** (`LOG` with a translated sentence): no limits offered, not delivered after two seconds, not readable, the depth not readable, or a view that got no stereo buffers on a desktop that offers them. A person who ticks Enable 3D where the desktop offers no stereo buffers gets a dialog; a 2D screen with the default on gets one view and nothing said.
8. **Trace for the proof:** a Qt logging category, off by default (`QT_LOGGING_RULES="openscad.stereo.debug=true"`), printing each frame's limits, `k`, `C`, the nearest and farthest distances, the separation and the predicted extremes in pixels.

## The closed-form scene and what it measured

A scene whose depths are known lets every number be predicted before the capture: `$vpr = [90, 0, 0]` (the front view: `Camera::setVpr` stores `90 - x`, so 90 gives no object rotation), `$vpt = [0, 0, 0]`, `$vpd = 200`, `$vpf = 22.5`; a red cube 16 x 1 x 16 at y = -30 (near face at distance 169.5), a blue one at y = +20 (near face 219.5) and a grey plate at y = +40 (near face 239).
With the view 753 pixels high, `k = (753 / 2) / tan(11.25°) = 1892.79`, whatever the width: the field of view is vertical, so a wider view keeps the same `k` and the same separation.
At the limits 10 sunk and 4 popped the popped side decides, `t = 4 / (k (200/169.5 - 1)) = 0.0117443`, and the predictions are red -4.000, blue +1.975, plate +3.627 pixels; at 20 and 8, -8.000, +3.950, +7.255; orthographic at 10 and 4 (`k = 9.46397` from the trace), -4.000, +2.557, +5.115.

## Traps

- **Show Axes on an axis-aligned view flattens the pair.** **Symptom:** the front view with the axes shown gives a separation of 0.000235 and eyes almost equal. **Cause:** the camera looks along the y axis, so the axis line runs through the eyes and its near end is cut at the near plane (distance 20); counted like any other pixel, it decides the fit. **How it was found:** the trace's `nearest=20`. **Fix:** none in the fit, which follows the rule that every pixel stays inside the limits; the default camera (55, 0, 25) puts no axis through the eye (the CSG example with the axes shown keeps the full depth).
- **OpenSCAD opens a file without a preview.** **Symptom:** an empty view, and the scene's `$vpr`, `$vpt` and `$vpd` not applied. **Fix:** send F5 (evdev 63) after the window is mapped and wait for the preview.
- **The D-Bus input driver is experimental.** **Symptom:** `org.openscad.OpenSCAD was not provided` although `[input] enableDriverDBUS=true`. **Cause:** non-experimental builds have no `--enable` for it. **Fix:** drive the view with fake input and prove each step by pixels.
- **A drag through fake input does nothing when its steps come at once.** **Fix:** waits between press, motions and release (`p:272 s:100 m:... s:60 ... r:272`).
- **Translucent objects write depth.** OpenSCAD never calls `glDepthMask`, so a `%` or `#` object counts at its front face, not at a weighted mean.
- **The analysis' own traps:** OpenSCAD draws the negative axes dashed, and a repeating pattern matches at several shifts with no cost: take a block only when no other shift, away from the best one's neighbours, matches nearly as well (the first zero of `argmin` is the most negative shift, -20 px here). An exact match is a whole shift: a parabola through unequal neighbours moved two equal eyes by up to a third of a pixel. A capture taken between two geometries of the view (a dock hidden) needs that geometry, not the wire's last one.
- **OpenSCAD dies under memcheck before `main`.** **Symptom:** `CGAL::Interval_nt<false>::Test_runtime_rounding_modes` aborts in a static initializer, after a dozen "Mismatched free() / delete / delete []". **Cause:** CGAL checks at start that the CPU rounds as it asks ("Wrong rounding: did you forget the -frounding-math option", `Interval_nt.h` in CGAL 6.2.1), and Valgrind supports "only the IEEE default mode (round to nearest)" for most floating-point operations (its manual, Limitations); the mismatches are mimalloc's allocator beside Valgrind's. **Fix:** memcheck a build of the same source with `-DUSE_MIMALLOC=OFF` and `-DCGAL_DISABLE_ROUNDING_MATH_CHECK` (upstream's own settings for its Emscripten build); the shipped build keeps both. Give the program llvmpipe for GL and lavapipe for Vulkan (`VK_DRIVER_FILES`): Qt Multimedia's FFmpeg plugin probes a Vulkan device at start, and NVIDIA's driver then reports allocations of size zero.
- **Closing a window read a destroyed rubber band (upstream's fault, found by that memcheck).** **Symptom:** "Invalid read of size 1" in `RubberBandManager::isVisible()` from `MainWindow::eventFilter()`, inside a block freed by `~RubberBandManager`, after Ctrl+Q. **Cause:** the window filters its own events; its rubber band manager is a child of the window, so its `~QObject`, which runs after its `QRubberBand` member is gone, sends `ChildRemoved` through that filter. **Fix:** `~MainWindow` removes its own filter and the find panel's, as upstream's #7005 does for the docks (patch 0005).
- **The 2D control compared two different layouts.** **Symptom:** the package with Enable 3D off put its 3D view at x 882, 1035 wide; upstream's own build at x 433, 1484 wide. **Cause:** OpenSCAD finds its resources beside its binary (`../share/openscad`), and the two folders differ: Debian's rules (kept) drop the bundled fonts. **Fix:** run upstream's unchanged binary from a folder whose `share/openscad` is a copy of the package's, so the binary is the only difference.
- **Vertical disparity read on slanted edges.** **Symptom:** a whole-area row comparison picked a one-row offset after an orbit, and a block search over rows and shifts picked rows other than 0 for half the blocks. **Cause:** a block holding one straight slanted edge matches as well one row up with a sideways shift (the aperture problem). **Fix:** prove it on an axis-aligned view (407 of 407 blocks best at row offset 0) and by the construction: the eye moves along the camera's right vector and the frustum shifts sideways only, so a point's height on the screen is the same in both eyes.
