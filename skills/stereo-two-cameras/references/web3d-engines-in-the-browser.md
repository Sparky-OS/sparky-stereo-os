# Measuring any web 3D engine's two eyes in the stereo browser

The harness of ORDER 477a: one scene written in each engine's own way, run through the browser's two routes, both eyes captured at Stereo KWin and compared with a prediction.
It extends [x3d-in-the-browser.md](x3d-in-the-browser.md), which holds the first recipe (X_ITE, X3DOM).

## The recipe

1. **The scene.** The four-box scene of x3d-in-the-browser.md as data (viewpoint at world (0,0,10) looking down -Z, vertical field of view 0.7854 rad, 800x450 canvas, four unlit boxes whose front faces are 2, 5, 12 and 30 m away, each 0.08 of its distance wide, pure yellow, cyan, magenta, green).
   Every engine builds it with unlit materials and no tone mapping, inside a page with a heading, text, a button and a table (the HTML around must be identical in both eyes), the canvas inside a `#cde` wrap so the canvas can be found in the capture.
2. **A dolly.** Put the camera in a group at (0,0,10) and the boxes in world coordinates: a WebXR session replaces the camera's own pose but keeps its parent, so the dolly is the XR origin (three.js, A-Frame rig, PlayCanvas parent entity, Babylon's camera position is copied into its XR camera).
3. **Reference space.** Ask for `local` through the engine's own API (three `renderer.xr.setReferenceSpaceType('local')`, A-Frame `webxr="referenceSpaceType: local"`, Babylon `referenceSpaceType`, PlayCanvas `XRSPACE_LOCAL`, SparkXr `referenceSpaceType`); the engines' defaults ask for `local-floor`, which the browser's flat mode answers with a viewer height (see the traps).
4. **Libraries from npm tarballs**, version pinned, the registry's sha512 checked, extracted into a new empty folder, served from there; a bundler (esbuild) in a Node container for react-three-fiber; the test container runs `--network none`, so nothing outside is fetched.
5. **Tap, change nothing.** Wrap `navigator.xr.requestSession`, `XRSession.prototype.requestReferenceSpace` and `XRFrame.prototype.getViewerPose` so every frame's views are recorded per reference-space kind (projection matrix, inverse transform), plus the layer's framebuffer and viewports.
6. **One real click** on the engine's own button, located by the page (`window.__xrButtonRect()`): WebDriver pointer actions, never `element.click()`.
7. **Prediction.** Project the eight corners of each box through the recorded views, take the convex hull of the projected corners; the silhouette's centroid is what the mask measures.
   A hard-edged raster (no antialiasing) is predicted by the centroid of the pixels whose centre lies inside the hull; a soft one by the area centroid.
   Without that distinction the error floor is 0.3 px and hides real differences.
8. **Measurement.** Per colour, a coverage-weighted centroid (background from a ring around the mask), left and right; disparity = left minus right, vertical, position error, and for the hack and the 2D control the canvas origin found from the wrap colour in the capture (the browser window's own offset is about 11 by 5 px, so the page rectangle is not where the canvas is).
9. **Controls that must fail:** the 2D control (`webgl.stereo-camera.enabled` false, eyes equal), the eyes swapped (sign of the measured disparity inverted against the prediction), the session's views replaced by one view (a three.js page that copies the left camera onto the right, `?mono=1`).
10. **A layer that is not the capture's size** (Spark's SparkXr asks for half the framebuffer): the prediction is scaled by capture eye size over viewport size; KWin does the one resize.

## What was found (2026-11-08, browser 6.7.5 +stereo3d11+lc1, Stereo KWin +stereo3d46, three.js 0.187.0, A-Frame 1.8.0, Babylon.js 9.35.0, PlayCanvas 2.23.3, react-three-fiber 9.8.2, model-viewer 4.4.0, Spark 2.4.0, CesiumJS 1.147.0)

- **WebXR route, flat TV or output and flat headset.** three.js, A-Frame, Babylon.js, react-three-fiber and Spark draw each eye with the session's views: disparity within 0.06 px of the prediction, vertical 0.03 px or less, 0 swapped boxes (PlayCanvas within 0.24 px in flat TV mode and 0.48 px in flat headset mode: it replaces the session's near and far, 0.05 and 999.9 instead of 0.1 and 1000, and rebuilds the matrix). All of them drop the authored 45 degree angle for the session's (25.45 degrees flat TV, 21.91 degrees flat headset). The zero-parallax plane is 1.0 m in both modes, so a scene authored 2 to 30 m away sits behind the screen.
- **Camera hack route.** three.js, A-Frame, Babylon.js, PlayCanvas, react-three-fiber and Spark all get parallax and the declaration (`set_content(3)`), the authored framing kept (0.06 px, Spark 0.65 px), vertical 0.01 px, the HTML around identical. The plane is 5.0 times the engine's near plane (0.1 gives 0.50 m, Babylon's default 1 gives 5.0 m, A-Frame's default 0.005 gives 0.04 m), so with the library's defaults the plane lies in front of the scene or at its edge and every box is behind the screen at about -12 to -16 px. CesiumJS computes its near plane from the visible tiles (about 1.95 m), so its plane lands at 9.8 m, inside the scene, with +58.8 to -11.0 px.
- **No route.** model-viewer draws into a 2D canvas copied from a shared renderer, so the hack finds no WebGL canvas (nothing declared, 0 px parallax) and its AR button is hidden (`canActivateAR` false; only `immersive-ar` appears in its code). CesiumJS has no WebXR code (no file names `requestSession`, `navigator.xr` or `XRSession`) and its own `scene.useWebVR` is a split screen of its own, not WebXR. WebGPU canvases (three.js WebGPURenderer, Babylon.js WebGPUEngine, backend confirmed WebGPUBackend) are drawn but never declared: 0 px parallax under the hack. three.js's WebGPURenderer XR path throws on the missing `XRSession.enabledFeatures`; Babylon's WebGPU engine clicked its button and asked for no session.

## Traps

- **A canvas with `image-rendering: pixelated` is black** (found by ORDER 477a, cause and fix by ORDER 482). Symptom: CesiumJS, which sets that style, draws correctly (`readPixels` after `scene.render()` shows the boxes, 0 GL errors) but the capture of the canvas is black in 2D, hack and declared; the same on a plain three.js page with `canvas{image-rendering:pixelated}`, and a video with the style is solid green. Cause: the browser's Wayland native compositor lock (see [native-linux, references/browser-pixelated-canvas.md](../../native-linux/references/browser-pixelated-canvas.md)); fixed in the browser by the order482 branch of Sparky-OS/waterfox (page-side `canvas.style.imageRendering='auto'` is no longer needed). Found by moving one style at a time, then by reading which surfaces reached KWin (`WAYLAND_DEBUG=1`).
- **Engines that ask for `local-floor`** (three.js VRButton, A-Frame by default) get a viewer 1.24 m above the origin in the flat modes; the scene built for the origin drops out of the 25 degree view. Symptom: the nearest box missing from both eyes. Cause: the recorded views of the `local-floor` space carry the height. Fix: ask for `local`, or predict from the recorded views of the space the engine used.
- **A library button picked by tag name** (`document.querySelector('button')`) finds the page's own button; look it up by its text or class.
- **Babylon.js refuses WebXR on `http://127.0.0.1`.** Symptom: `createDefaultXRExperienceAsync` throws "WebXR can only be served over HTTPS". Cause: it allows only the host name `localhost` over http. Fix: serve and browse `http://localhost:PORT`.
- **react-three-fiber's default `Canvas` tone-maps (ACES)**; pure colours come out pale and the masks miss them. Use `flat` for the measurement and say so.
- **A float preference written as a number is ignored** (see the first reference).
- **Cesium's `PerspectiveFrustum.fov` is the angle of the larger side**, so a 45 degree vertical angle at 16:9 is 72.7 degrees.
- **A page rectangle is not the canvas in the capture:** find the canvas from a coloured wrap.

## A finished three.js page with a sky and a post stack (ORDER 494, 2026-11-10)

Cosmic Shore's screensaver (three.js 0.181.2, MIT; an EffectComposer with bloom and a Panini pass, an instanced Garland, a baked cube-map sky) run from its own build, unchanged, in the same bench (browser +stereo3d10, Stereo KWin +stereo3d46, Mesa on an AMD render node).
It has no WebXR code, so only the camera hack applies; the flat modes need a page that asks for an `immersive-vr` session.

### Recipe for a page you did not write

1. Build it from its lockfile in a Node container (`npm ci --ignore-scripts`, then its own build), serve the `dist` folder from disk with the standard library's HTTP server inside the test container (no network).
2. Freeze the page with its own parameters, not with a patch: a frame cap near zero (`?fps=0.01` here) draws the first frame only, so the capture and the state you read belong to one frame, and the seed makes it repeat.
3. Read the page's state through whatever it exposes (here `?debug` puts `__camera` and `__crystal` on `window`; the crystal's `object.parent` is the scene, so the camera's matrices and every instance matrix of the instanced mesh come out as plain numbers).
4. Predict with the hack's formula, which patches the projection matrix: each eye's clip x becomes x -/+ s (w - C), s the separation (0.02 of the width by default), C five times the camera's near plane. Add every later pass that remaps the picture (here Panini, inverted numerically) before comparing.
5. Measure by block matching: a patch of the left capture around each prediction found in the right capture (normalised cross correlation, parabola for the sub pixel), keeping matches above 0.9 whose next peak is lower by 0.03. Disparity is left x minus right x, negative behind the screen.
6. Controls: the 2D case (eyes identical, difference 0), the eyes swapped (every sign flips, error 52 px against 0.2), and a dense disparity map of the whole picture, so a layer you did not predict shows up as its own cluster.

Numbers: 60 prisms at 241 to 1336 units, predicted -27.6 to -23.2 px, measured within 0.18 px rms; the vessel at 25 units (-24.96 predicted, -24.93 measured), the crystal at 378 units (-27.51, -27.52); `webgl.stereo-camera.convergence` 50.0 moved the vessel to +0.14 px (-0.33 predicted).

### Traps

- **A `scene.background` cube map stands in front of the screen.** Symptom: the dense map has two clusters, the scene at -25 px and the sky at +60 to +140 px; the nebula and the sun glow come out of the screen while everything in front of them sits behind it. Cause: three.js draws a background as a box of side 1 centred on the camera with `gl_Position.z = gl_Position.w`, so its clip w is 0.5 to 0.87, nearer than the plane (5 x near = 2.5 here), and the hack's x + s (w - C) puts it in front. A box draw's disparity predicted from w alone matched 665 close-up blocks within -0.1 px mean, 3 px std (the cube's own baking pass through the hack did not matter). Raising `convergence` makes it worse: at 200 (plane 100) the sky left the picture altogether (black in both eyes), because the shift grows with C / w. Fix on the page, 23 lines: draw the sky as a box of half side 3000 (inside the far plane), `BackSide`, `depthTest` and `depthWrite` off, `renderOrder` -1000, following the camera. The sky then sits with the far scene (all 1284 blocks in one band), the 2D picture is identical (mean difference 0.0004 of 255), and a plane set to the scene (convergence 1000) spreads the garland over -17 to +30 px. Found by the dense map's second cluster and by a left and right capture that showed the sun 100 px apart.
- **With the default plane a large scene is a flat wall.** The plane is 5 x near; a scene of units 25 to 1300 away has every part at 1 - C / w of the far parallax: 4.4 px of range in 25 (the whole Garland), so it reads as one sunk plane. The page's near plane or the preference has to follow the scene (ORDER 473t's scene-derived plane is the browser's side).
- **The window is not the canvas.** The capture of a kiosk window is 1280x1024 while the canvas is 1264x1014 at (11, 5): find the corner from the page's dark background against the border, or every prediction is 11 px off.
- **Marionette's script world is not the page's.** `window.requestAnimationFrame = ...` set from a script does not reach the page, `exportFunction` is undefined and the page's CSP (`script-src 'self'`) refuses an inline script; reading page properties works. Freeze with the page's own frame cap instead.
- **Vertical disparity read as 0.7 px** is the integer row step of the matcher, not the picture: use a sub pixel step before calling it a defect.
- **Large shifts break the simple prediction.** At convergence 1000 the same 60 prisms were predicted -17 to +26 px and measured -17 to +30, but the point by point error was 14 px rms (40 matches, ambiguous patches over a wide range): the range and sign agree, the 0.2 px agreement of the default plane is not shown there.
