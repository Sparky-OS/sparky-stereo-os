# Measuring an X3D scene's two eyes in the browser

Measured 2026-11-08 on the edition's browser (Waterfox-based, 6.7.5 +stereo3d10) with X_ITE 16.7.1 (MIT, Holger Seelig) and X3DOM 1.8.3 (MIT or GPL-3.0), each from its own npm release (checked against the registry's SHA-512), under a private Stereo KWin with a stereo virtual output, Mesa on an AMD render node.

## The recipe

1. **A scene with known geometry.** A Viewpoint at `0 0 10` (the X3D default pose, ISO/IEC 19775-1 23.3.1; X3D units are metres, 4.3.6) and four unlit boxes (emissive colour only, headlight off) whose front faces are 2, 5, 12 and 30 m away, each 0.08 of its distance wide, in four pure colours.
   Place them for the narrow view a flat WebXR session gives (about 25 degrees tall), or the near and far boxes leave the picture.
2. **The same markup for both libraries:** `<x3d-canvas><X3D><Scene>...` for X_ITE, `<x3d><scene>...` (explicit closing tags) for X3DOM. A page with no library script renders nothing: `<X3D>` and `<x3d-canvas>` are unknown elements, and the measurement says so (0 canvases).
3. **Three cases per library, each in a fresh container and session:** the 2D control (`webgl.stereo-camera.enabled` false: the two captures equal, disparity 0), the camera hack (pref true), and WebXR (`dom.vr.enabled`, `dom.vr.webxr.enabled`, `dom.vr.flat.enabled`, `permissions.default.xr` 1, `dom.vr.flat.mode` 0 = flat TV or output, 1 = flat headset).
4. **WebXR needs a real click on the library's own button:** X_ITE's is `.x_ite-private-xr-button` inside the `x3d-canvas` shadow root (it appears over the canvas), X3DOM's is `.x3dom-vr`. Move a real pointer over the canvas first, then click at the button's centre with WebDriver pointer actions (this is what worked; an element click and a script click were not tried).
5. **Tap the session, change nothing:** wrap `navigator.xr.requestSession` and `XRSession.prototype.requestAnimationFrame` in the page so each frame's `views` (projection matrix and inverse transform) and the base layer's viewports are recorded. The prediction is then each box's front-face centre (viewpoint at the XR origin) projected through the recorded views; disparity in pixels is the left eye's x minus the right's, so negative means behind the screen.
6. **Measure:** capture both eyes (ScreenShot2), find each colour's centroid per eye inside the canvas (in a flat WebXR session the whole screen), report disparity, vertical shift (0 expected) and, for a page with text around the canvas, the difference outside the canvas (0 expected).
7. **Zero parallax by arithmetic:** the distance at which the two recorded views agree (bisection on the projected x difference) is the screen plane; compare it with the preference that sets it (`dom.vr.flat.screen-plane-mm`).

## What was found

- X_ITE and X3DOM both use `navigator.xr.requestSession("immersive-vr")` and the `local` reference space and draw each eye with the views the session gives, so flat WebXR gives each eye its own view on both: measured against the prediction, within 0.3 px at the default plane (1 m) and the 3.5 mm flat TV interaxial, 0.2 px for the 8.7 mm flat headset one, 2 px with the plane at 10 m (the near box is partly clipped there).
- The Viewpoint's `fieldOfView` (23.4.6) is not used: the view angle is the display's (25.4 degrees tall in flat TV mode, 21.9 in flat headset mode against the scene's 45), so the authored framing changes and boxes grow 2.5 times.
- X3D has no zero-parallax plane, so metres 1 to 1 against a 1 m screen plane put a scene that is 2 to 30 m away wholly behind the screen (-4 to -7.5 px at 1280 wide). With the plane at the Viewpoint's distance to its `centerOfRotation` (10 m, `dom.vr.flat.screen-plane-mm` 10000) the same boxes read +29.6, +7.1, -1.5, -5.5 px against +31.5, +7.9, -1.3, -5.2 predicted.
- The camera hack keeps the author's framing and the HTML around (outside-canvas difference 0.000) but puts the screen plane at 5 times the library's near plane: X_ITE's near is 0.125 m (plane about 0.6 m, all boxes behind the screen, -11 to -15.5 px of 800, so 2 m and 30 m differ by 4.5 px), X3DOM computes its near from the scene (1.4 m, plane about 7 m: +59.6, +9.0, -11.5, -19.5). `webgl.stereo-camera.convergence` is a multiple of the near plane.

## The prediction without a session

Where a point at distance Z shows against a screen plane at distance d, in pixels of a picture `width` wide: `-interaxial * (Z - d) / Z / (2 d tan (hfov / 2)) * width` (left minus right, so behind the screen is negative).
With the flat TV mode's recorded values (interaxial 3.47 mm at a 1 m plane, a view 31.5 degrees wide, 1280 pixels) this gives -3.94, -6.30, -7.22, -7.61 for 2, 5, 12 and 30 m: the session's predicted column to 0.01 px.
The interaxial grows with the plane: the 10 m column (+31.5, +7.87, -1.31, -5.25) comes out only with 34.7 mm, 3.47 mm for each metre of screen distance. A prediction that keeps 3.47 mm at 10 m is ten times too small.
The camera hack is on by default (`webgl.stereo-camera.enabled`), so an X_ITE canvas in any page, a local file included, reaches KWin with two views; for a 2D control turn it off.

## The camera hack's prediction, and the outline of a box

The hack's formula is in the browser's source (`dom/canvas/WebGLStereoCamera.h`, branch `stereo3d` of Sparky-OS/waterfox): each eye's clip x is `x -/+ s (w - C)`, with s the separation as a share of the view's width (Normal is 2 %), C the zero-parallax distance (`webgl.stereo-camera.convergence` times the camera's near distance) and w the depth. In pixels, left minus right: `s W (C / Z - 1)`.
Predict what is measured: the measurement is the centroid of a box's pixels, so the prediction is the centroid of its outline (the convex hull of its eight corners projected for each eye), not its front-face centre.
With that, seven cases on X_ITE and X3DOM (defaults and a 10 m plane, the hack, flat TV and flat headset) agree with the captures within 0.46 px; with the front-face centre the 10 m cases were 2 px off.
A scene-derived plane for X_ITE under the hack is `convergence = distance / near` ("80.0" for 10 m with X_ITE's near of 0.125 m); under flat WebXR it is `dom.vr.flat.screen-plane-mm`. X3D itself has no field to carry it.

## Traps

- **A float preference written as a number is ignored.** Symptom: `webgl.stereo-camera.convergence` 80.0 in user.js changed nothing. Cause: Gecko stores float preferences as strings. Fix: `"80.0"`. Found by the identical numbers of two runs.
- **A model/x3d type is not shown.** Navigating to a `.x3d`, `.x3dv` or `.x3dj` served as `model/x3d+xml`, `model/x3d+vrml` or `model/x3d+json` times out and leaves about:blank (an external handler, nothing drawn); served as `application/xml` it opens the XML tree.
- **JSON in a shell word:** a preference object passed through an unquoted variable breaks at its space; write it compact.
- **Measure the whole screen in a flat WebXR session:** the library's canvas is resized to the session's layer, so its page rectangle is not where the views are.
- **A still scene shows the same picture to both eyes** (fixed in the browser by ORDER 473t, branch order473t of Sparky-OS/waterfox; the page-side workaround is no longer needed). Symptom: one canvas of a long page measures 0 px while animated canvases beside it are in stereo (`xite-o1.html`, 0.00 px). Cause: the host drew the page's frames once per eye only after the canvas's first present (`options.stereoCamera`), and a canvas that is below the fold is never presented, so the one frame the page drew was drawn with one camera. Not the cause, each ruled out by its own page: a scene built with `createX3DFromString`, a still scene as such, a rounded clip on the canvas, a small canvas, four canvases on one page. Fix: the client sends `SetStereoCamera` to the host as soon as it recognises the camera and the canvas has no renderer yet. Found by reading `WebGLContext::Present` against `StereoCameraDraws()`: the flag the draws look at changed only at present. Result on the fixed build: -11.0, -14.0, -15.03, -15.99 px (the pre-fix canvas with one field written after the scroll gave -11.01, -14.02, -15.00, -15.53).
- **`gfx.webrender.compositor.force-enabled` false removes the declaration.** No `set_content`, eyes equal, for still and animated canvases alike. The package sets it true; a test profile must not turn it off.
- **A plain 2D output cannot be proved in the virtual rig.** ScreenShot2 answers with both eyes whenever a window holds stereo content, also on an output in a 2D mode, and the virtual backend has no scanout to read. What can be measured there: the HTML around the canvas is identical with the hack on and off, and the left eye's picture differs from the hack-off picture only inside the canvas (the camera sits half a separation to the left).

## 2D outputs, the scene's plane, and what a still canvas cannot be asked (ORDER 473t, 2026-11-10)

Measured on a dev build of branch order473t (on 6be1abb34e), X_ITE 16.7.1 and X3DOM 1.8.3, the four-box scene (2, 5, 12 and 30 m), private Stereo KWin, ScreenShot2, 800x450 canvas.

### The left eye is the page's own camera (2D outputs)

- Rule in KWin's source: a window that declares full side by side on an output without eyes is painted with `StereoEye::None`, and `ItemRenderer` maps that to the left eye (`itemrenderer_opengl.cpp`: `context->stereoEye == StereoEye::Right ? Right : Left`), whose quads are the left half of the buffer (`SurfaceItem::eyeQuads`, `scene/surfaceitem.cpp`). A 2D screen copying a 3D one takes the left eye too (STEREO3D.md, replicas). So whatever the left half holds is what a 2D output shows.
- With the page's camera between the eyes (shifts -s and +s) that picture was half a separation off the page's own: 2829 pixels of the 1280x1024 capture differed from the hack-off page (largest channel difference 191).
- Now `webgl.stereo-camera.left-eye-is-page-camera` (default true) draws the left eye with shift 0 and the right with 2s. The parallax between the eyes is the same (`s W (1 - C/z)`, left minus right, negative behind the screen), the left half is the page's own picture: 0 of 1310720 pixels differ (control, the hack-off page captured twice: 0). With the pref false: 2829 again, so the test can fail.
- Why not "one central camera while the output cannot show stereo": the window's depth state (`awake`) is false for a 2D output and also for every window behind the active one, so the camera would change at each focus change, and a canvas that draws once (on demand) keeps the frame it had until the page draws again. The left eye as the page's camera needs no state and no redraw.
- Prove it by comparing the left capture of the hack page with the hack-off capture (a few lines of numpy: the count of pixels whose largest channel difference is above 0, and that largest difference, inside and outside the canvas), on the `capable-2d` rig and on the `capable-anaglyph` one. ScreenShot2 answers with both eyes in either rig, so this is the proof by KWin's rule plus the left half, not a DRM scanout.

### The scene's zero-parallax plane

- **What it reads.** `webgl.stereo-camera.scene-plane` (default true): the depth buffer the page's scene was drawn into is read now and then (the first four presents, then one in 64) and reduced to 64 by 36 samples; the eye distance of a sample is `numerator / (2 z - 1 - a)` with `a = row2 . row3` and `numerator = m14 - a m15` of the camera matrix (`StereoCamera::depthA`, `depthNumerator`: the same row product that gives the near distance), samples at the far plane are not things. The nearest and the farthest are the 2nd and 98th percentile. Measured: nearest 2.000 and farthest 30.246 (X_ITE), 30.000 (X3DOM) for boxes at 2 and 30 m.
- **Where the plane goes.** Where the nearest and the farthest have equal and opposite parallax (parallax goes with 1/distance): `2 n f / (n + f)`, here 3.75 m. Then no nearer than the person's popped limit lets the nearest thing come: the parallax of a point at distance z is `separation * (plane / z - 1)` of the view's width, the limit is in pixels of a view 1920 wide, so `plane <= n (1 + popped / (1920 separation))`. The limits are the window's (`nsDisplayStereoDepth::LimitsFor`, compositor first, libstereo-policy's default for the display otherwise). With the rig's limits (popped about 4 px at 1920) and a 2 % separation the plane is 2.208 m: the near box pops +1.5 px (the +13 package put X3DOM's near box at +59.6 px, 36 times the limit). Predicted against measured with the outline-centroid prediction: worst 0.46 px (X_ITE), 0.30 px (X3DOM).
- **A flat WebXR session** (`dom.vr.flat.scene-plane`, default true; `dom.vr.flat.screen-plane-mm` set by the person still wins): the session's depth range goes to the host in the present options, the host reads the page's last framebuffer that has a depth buffer, `XRSession::GetWindowViews` takes the plane from it, clamped by `nearest * (1 + popped / sunk)` of the display's depth. X_ITE: plane 5.589 m (interaxial 19.4 mm), measured +13.5, +0.52, -4.02, -6.51 px against +13.3, +0.61, -4.33, -6.45 predicted from the recorded views (worst 0.31 px). The session's metres are not the author's: X_ITE's scene read 4.0 to 64 m there for boxes authored at 2 to 30 m.
- **Limit, said:** the first frame uses the camera's own plane (5 times the near distance). A canvas that draws exactly once (xite-o1) keeps it: its disparities are the old -11 to -16 px. The plane follows the scene from the second presented frame on.

### Traps

- **Reading a page's depth: `readPixels` says INVALID_OPERATION (0x502) and the canvas has no depth.** Symptom: the log says "no scene in the depth buffer (the canvas)" for every frame. Cause: X_ITE (and engines with post-processing) draw the scene into framebuffers of their own, multisampled, and the canvas's default framebuffer has no depth (`depth: false`); a multisampled framebuffer cannot be read, and a depth blit needs equal formats. Fix: remember the page framebuffer with a depth attachment that the draws went into (`WebGLContext::DrawPerEye`), ask the attachment's bit size, resolve by `blitFramebuffer` into a single-sample renderbuffer of the same format, read that. Found by `MOZ_LOG=sync,WebGLStereo:4` printing "present two eyes: 0 draws per eye, 7 into the page's framebuffers" (nothing was drawn into the canvas's own) and the GL error code.
- **`MOZ_LOG_FILE` stays empty.** The probe ends the browser with a forced quit, which does not flush the log. Fix: `MOZ_LOG=sync,WebGLStereo:4`.
- **A new synchronous IPDL message is refused:** "New sync IPC messages must be reviewed by an IPC peer and recorded in sync-messages.ini". Use `async Name() returns (...)` (the answer comes in a `->Then`, the caller keeps the last answer).
- **A dev build's `dist/bin` does not run alone:** `Couldn't load XPCOM`. Mount the object folder and the source tree read-only at their own paths and run the binary from its real `dist/bin` path (a mount at `/b` breaks the relative symlinks). A harness that does it starts the browser in a container with `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` set to that real path.
- **A first full dev build crashed one compile with exit 135 (SIGBUS) and make stopped.** Starting the build again finished it; nothing in the source was at fault. About 65 minutes of compile on 4 threads for a first build, 3 minutes for a change in `dom/canvas`.
- **The camera hack's default:** the source's `webgl.stereo-camera.enabled` is false; the package's `defaults/pref/package-prefs.js` sets it true for every site, and the canvas context menu shows "On" until a site permission says otherwise.
