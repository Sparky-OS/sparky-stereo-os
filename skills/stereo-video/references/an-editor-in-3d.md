# An editor in 3D: Kdenlive and MLT (ORDER 61e, 2026-11-21)

Part of the [`stereo-video`](../SKILL.md) skill.

Check freshness first: written on 2026-11-21 from Kdenlive 26.08.1-1+stereo3d10+lc1 and MLT 7.40.0-1+stereo3d9+lc1, and on 2026-11-22 for Kdenlive +stereo3d11 and MLT +stereo3d10, then Kdenlive +stereo3d12 and MLT +stereo3d11, then Kdenlive +stereo3d13 and MLT +stereo3d12 (the edition's build tree, `sparky-os/packages/kdenlive-stereo-61e`; ORDER 61e, four milestones, the third as 61f, the fourth as 61g with its continuation), on the stereo layer 1.23.2 and Stereo KWin +stereo3d59.

An editor shows media, so it is a player for its monitors and a writer for its renders.
Its 3D options are the contract's: Enable 3D, the render resolution per eye (the project's resolution), a shortcut to Display Settings, and for each clip the input corrections.
Each part below has its own place and its own proof.

### The default, and how it is decided

- **Where the default lives:** the setting exists only in the edition's patches, so its default is set in an edition-only patch of the program, not in `/etc/xdg`. Both keep a person's choice, because KConfig writes a value that differs from the default (below); the patch keeps one place and no new conffile.
- **What decides 3D:** the menu offers 3D only when the compositor announces `kde_stereo_content_manager_v1` with `wl_subcompositor`, `wp_viewporter` and `wl_shm` (`StereoLayer::isSupported()`). Stereo KWin always announces it, also with only 2D outputs, where KWin shows the left view (Daniel, 2026-10-14: "our defaults are 3d always, when in a 2d monitor, we display left side"). On any other compositor the menu has no 3D entry and nothing is said.
- **X11:** the 3D area is a Wayland surface. Kdenlive on xcb under Stereo KWin's Xwayland (`stereo_supported_x11() > 0`) says once that the monitor shows 3D only on Wayland.
- **A monitor that cannot show 3D** (on X11, or after the compositor refused the declaration) shows what Enable 3D off shows, the left eye whole and at its own shape, with nothing declared, and its sentence says so ("It shows the left eye's view, flat."; Kdenlive patch 0025, the contract's "A program never stretches or squeezes an eye"). Up to +stereo3d12 it showed the packed frame, each eye squeezed to half its width, and said "It shows the packed frame as it is." The cause was one condition: `leftEye` was true for Left Eye Only and for off, never for 3D chosen without a 3D area; it is now true whenever the picture is 3D and no 3D area shows it.

### The program's 3D options, and what "off" shows

- **Where:** each monitor's menu (its toolbar's overflow, More Options, Stereoscopic 3D): Enable 3D, Left Eye Only, Display Settings…, and in the project monitor Packing of the Project. +stereo3d6 to +stereo3d8 had Preview (Packed Frame as It Is, Left Eye Only, 3D), Packing of the Project and Right Eye First there.
- **Enable 3D** is the stereo layer's action (`Stereo::Enable3DAction`, its words and catalogue). The setting keeps its values (`stereo3d_preview`, `stereo3d_clip_preview`): 0 off, 1 the left eye, 2 3D; a choice saved before reads the same.
- **Off** shows the left eye of a 3D picture, whole and at its own shape, and declares nothing (Kdenlive patch 0024): the stereo contract says "A program never stretches or squeezes an eye; KWin scales once, at the output", and a flat picture of a pair is its left eye, as a 2D screen shows it under Stereo KWin. Off and Left Eye Only show the same picture; only the switch differs. Up to +stereo3d11, off showed the frames as they are: in a 3D project the two eyes side by side, each squeezed to half its width. The picture programs' off (Gwenview, Koko, KPhotoAlbum, digiKam, Showfoto on the layer's `Enable3DAction`, ORDER 551c) loads each file as the layer's `View::Mono`, the file's own picture as a 2D program shows it (an MPO's first picture, its left eye).
- **Left Eye Only** is a view of one eye below the switch; choosing it enables 3D again, as in the picture programs (ORDER 551c).
- **Display Settings…** opens Display Configuration (`kcmshell6 kcm_kscreen` through `KIO::CommandLauncherJob`). Each screen's 3D format and its swap of the eyes are there. When the program cannot be started the person is told what did not start and where the screens' 3D formats are (`kcmshell6` comes from `libkf6kcmutils-bin`, which Plasma's desktop recommends).
- **No swap for everything.** The project-wide "Right Eye First" of +stereo3d6 to +stereo3d8 is gone. A project saved with it gets that packing with the right eye first as the input format of each video clip; it then takes its packing from its clips, and the project monitor says so once.

### The render resolution per eye

- A 3D project's resolution is one eye's. A 1920x1080 project whose packing comes from its clips renders 3840x1080: the render document's profile is doubled in width and in display aspect.
- Each clip's `stereo3d_unpack` fits its views to whole pictures (`target_views=full`). A clip with no filter in the project (a side-by-side clip of the project's own kind) gets one in the render.
- A clip whose frame is the doubled frame itself (3840x1080, square samples, left eye first) is taken as it is, decoded at its own depth.
- A project whose own frame already holds two whole views (3840x1080) is written as it is. So is a project whose packing the person declared by hand.
- **The monitors** work on the same frame as the render: in a 3D project the monitors' profile (`pCore->getMonitorProfile()`) is twice as wide, of twice the display aspect, and the timeline's `stereo3d_unpack` filters are made with it, so each eye is a whole picture of the project's resolution and KWin scales once. The preview resolution the person chooses scales that frame. A project whose own frame already holds two whole views keeps it.
  - The cost, measured playing a mixed 3D project for 20 s in the rig: Kdenlive took 0.52 of a processor core where +stereo3d9's half-width frame took 0.29 (AMD Renoir), 0.53 against 0.27 (NVIDIA RTX 3060, OpenGL on zink as the session gives it); KWin 0.02 to 0.04 either way; the GPU busy share 17 to 25 % on the AMD card either way (it also draws the station's desktop) and about 5 % on the NVIDIA card either way.
  - The monitor's area is fitted to the project's display aspect (`m_dar`, one eye's shape), so a picture of the frame of two eyes is the left half drawn in the whole area (Kdenlive patch 0024 tells the monitor its frames are a 3D project's pair, `setStereoPreview(..., pairFrame)`); up to +stereo3d11 both off (the whole frame) and Left Eye Only (the left half in half the area) showed an eye squeezed to half its width.
- **Plain presets** in a 3D project write the same doubled frame, marked: the resolution is one eye's whatever the preset.
- **Matroska's DisplayWidth** is one plane's. RFC 9559 section 18.10 (and Matroska's own notes, "Multi-planar and 3D Videos"): "The pixel count of the track (PixelWidth/PixelHeight) is the raw number of pixels (for example, 3840x1080 for full HD side by side), and the DisplayWidth/DisplayHeight in pixels is the number of pixels for one plane (1920x1080 for that full HD stream)." FFmpeg's muxer writes half the frame's width for side by side.
  - A 3840x1080 render carries 1920x1080, which is right.
  - The 1920x1080 renders of +stereo3d8 carried 960x1080 for views holding a 16:9 picture squeezed to half width: by the specification they said "show each eye at 960x1080", that is squeezed.

### A clip's input corrections

- **Every video clip's and still's Properties** offer the stereo layer's "3D Input Format": every entry of the layer's list, both eye orders, the anaglyph pairs and the picture-with-depth layouts.
  - The ids are the file marks' `Stereo3DLayout` words.
  - The words are the layer's, in its `Stereo::FileChoiceMenu` context, translated by the layer's own catalogue.
  - The list is the installed layer's, read when Kdenlive first needs it (`stereo_policy_input_format_count()` and `_at()`, stereo-policy 1.23); Kdenlive keeps no copy. Its packings map to MLT's as layouts 1, 2, 4 to 8; the others (both views in one block, the anaglyphs, the picture-with-depth layouts) are shown as one picture and say "(one view shown here)". The layer 1.23.2's list is 1.22's table with "automatic" first: no packing is new, so the proof that the list is read at run time is a rig library preloaded into Kdenlive that adds one entry (`scripts/tools/rig-input-entry.c`): it appears at the list's end and, chosen, makes the clip a pair.
- **Swap left and right eyes** is shown for a clip shown in 3D, or to take back a swap the clip has; **Save the correction into the file** for a clip shown in 3D (greyed while there is nothing to save) or holding a correction (a 3D clip set to Not 3D can be saved as 2D). A clip shown flat with no correction gets the list only, so an unmarked 3D clip can be declared, and nothing else.
- **Kept in the project** (`kdenlive:stereo3d_input`, `kdenlive:stereo3d_swap` on the clip, undoable), never in the file. The packing a clip is shown with (monitors, thumbnails, the project's conversion, renders) is the file's unless the person corrected it.
- **The save** writes the file's own mark through the layer's libstereo-av (`stereo_mark_save()`: Matroska's StereoMode with mkvpropedit, MP4's st3d in place). The library reads it back; Kdenlive then takes the correction off the clip and reads the clip again from the file.
  - A refusal says why: a container with no mark, a value the mark has not, the stream's own SEI declaring another format, an MP4 without room, mkvpropedit missing.
  - On a refusal the file is as it was and the correction stays in the project.
  - A still's correction is written by the layer (`Stereo::Picture::saveCorrection()`), which (1.23.2) writes only into JPEG pictures, a JPS's descriptor or an MPO; Kdenlive refuses a PNG itself with that reason.
  - After a save the clip is read again from the file and the project monitor shows its frame at once (patch 0021); +stereo3d9 showed black until the playhead moved.

### The source's depth

- MLT's `stereo3d_unpack` asked every producer for RGBA, 8 bits a sample, so a 10 bit 3D clip taken apart lost its depth: 256 luma levels in a row of a 10 bit ramp, where the source has 877.
- MLT 0015: asked for more than 8 bits a sample, the filter works the views in RGBA64 and hands back the format asked for, an opaque picture with no alpha mask. The result is 877 levels in each eye, through Kdenlive's headless render with its track compositing.
- Kdenlive itself keeps a 10 bit clip at 10 bits in a 2D project with no effect (877 levels with a 10 bit preset). Kdenlive's own presets are 8 bit.
- **A 3D project's render** (patch 0022): a 3D preset that names no pixel format writes the deepest video clip's depth (its stream's `pix_fmt`) up to its codec's: H.264 10 bits (ITU-T H.264 Annex A, High 10; its High 4:4:4 Predictive carries 14, which libx264 does not write), H.265 12 (Annex A, Main 10 and the format range extensions' Main 12; libx265 writes up to 12), FFV1 16 (RFC 9043; the 3D Matroska-FFV1 preset). Deeper than 10 bits MLT works the frame in RGBA64 (`mlt_image_format=rgba64`): with no format named, its encoder works yuv420p and a 12 bit request comes out with 8 bit steps.
- When the render writes fewer bits than the deepest clip (a 12 bit clip through H.264, a preset that names an 8 bit format), the person is told before it runs (the render dialog, and the command line's output); the render goes ahead.

### What a 3D render preset writes

```
f=mp4 movflags=+faststart strict=-1 vcodec=libx264 crf=%quality g=15 acodec=aac ab=%audiobitrate+'k' stereo3d=side_by_side
f=matroska vcodec=libx264 crf=%quality g=15 acodec=aac ab=%audiobitrate+'k' stereo3d=side_by_side
properties=x265-medium f=mp4 movflags=+faststart strict=-1 vcodec=libx265 crf=%quality:open-gop=0 acodec=aac ab=%audiobitrate+'k' stereo3d=side_by_side
properties=x265-medium f=matroska vcodec=libx265 crf=%quality:open-gop=0 acodec=aac ab=%audiobitrate+'k' stereo3d=side_by_side
```

MLT's avformat consumer turns `stereo3d` into frame side data (libx264 writes the H.264 SEI, D.2.26) and stream side data (Matroska `StereoMode`, RFC 9559 5.1.4.1.28.3; MP4 `st3d`).
A render with a 3D preset of a project with no 3D clip, or whose frames the person declared packed another way, is refused with the reason: a 2D video is never marked 3D.

### Clips of different packings in one project

Every clip whose frames hold both views gets `stereo3d_unpack` (`packing`, `invert`, `output=side_by_side`, `in_producer=1`, `target_views`) on its timeline producer, while the project's packing comes from its clips.
In a producer the filter takes the views out of the producer's own picture and fits each, keeping its shape, to its half of a frame of the profile's size: the one resize an editor makes of any clip.
`target_views=half` is for a profile whose frames carry half views squeezed to half their width (a 1920x1080 side by side profile, the shape the players' shared rule reads as half views); the view is fitted to a half shown twice as wide.
A clip already side by side, left eye first, with views of the project's kind keeps its frames as they are (no RGBA conversion).
Frame sequences are not taken apart in a producer.

### 2D elements in a 3D project

- "Faked 3D becomes stereo by doubling": in a 3D project a 2D clip, a title, a picture or a colour is the same picture in both eyes, at the screen plane (MLT's `stereo3d_unpack packing=mono` as the first filter of its timeline producer, patch 0019), in the monitors and the renders alike. Its transparency is kept, so a title over a 3D clip shows the clip's pair under it, the title at the same place in both eyes.
- Colours, pictures, titles and templates use a cut of the bin clip's own producer in a 2D project; in a 3D project they get producers of their own, which carry the filter (`ProjectClip::getTimelineProducer()`). When a project becomes 3D (or stops being), every clip's timeline instances are made again, and once more when the project has finished opening (`KdenliveDoc::checkStereo3DPairProject()`).

### Effects and compositions on each eye

- **The rule:** a 3D project's resolution is one eye's, so every geometry a person sets is in one eye's coordinates, as in a 2D project of the same size. Moving a clip 100 samples right moves it 100 samples right in both eyes; scaling keeps each eye in its own half; a crop crops each eye.
- **Where it lives: MLT's framework, once for every service.** `stereo3d_unpack` marks each frame of two eyes it makes (`stereo3d.pair`). When a filter's or a transition's `process` runs on a marked frame, the framework takes the entries it pushed on the frame's image stack and puts one get_image of its own in their place. That get_image runs the service once per eye: the picture under it is taken once, at twice the eye's width, and each run gets one eye of it as a picture of its own. A transition's second frame is taken once too, and each eye's run gets a stand-in frame holding that eye of it. The services keep the profile of one eye, so `mlt_profile_scale_width(profile, width)` is 1 at full size and the preview scale otherwise. A per-service patch would have been needed for each of about 200 services; the framework is one place.
- **Chains, not single services.** Services stacked directly on one another on a frame run together for each eye (the left eye through all of them, then the right), so what one leaves on the frame for the next belongs to the same eye: `mask_start` keeps a frame for `mask_apply`.
- **Each eye starts clean.** The frame's string properties are restored before the right eye's run, keeping what taking the picture under the services set: a qtblend leaves `qtblend_scalingx` for the next qtblend, and the right eye would otherwise take the left eye's run for an earlier qtblend.
- **One eye's size at set-up too.** While a wrapped service's `process` runs, the frame says one eye's media size: the view's own samples as the clip holds them (`stereo3d_unpack.view_width` and `_height`, MLT 0021), and on leaving what the service left, as two eyes of the frame: `crop` sizes itself at process time, and with Use Project Resolution off its amounts are the clip's own samples. Up to MLT +stereo3d11 the frame said half the frame of two eyes, so a 4:3 clip in a 16:9 project was cut by amounts read as 1920-sample amounts (measured: picture 260 to 1659 where the 2D control has 322 to 1598).
- **What stays whole.** A producer's normalizers (`_loader`) and `stereo3d_unpack` itself act on the frame of two eyes. So do services that keep earlier pictures: their memory would mix the eyes if they were given each eye in turn. The factory marks them `_stereo3d.whole_frame`: 1 where the frame as a whole gives what each eye apart would (freeze, temporal blends, deinterlacers), 2 where it cannot act on each eye (stabilizers, trackers, temporal denoisers that also reach over neighbouring samples, a zoom that accumulates). Kdenlive's panel says so for kind 2 in a 3D project. A frame without the mark, every 2D project's, takes the code paths it always took.
- **The render keeps one eye's profile.** MLT's consumer writes its `width` into its profile, and in a render the consumer shares the document's profile with every service. So the render document keeps the project's profile, each clip's `stereo3d_unpack` makes the frame of two profile-sized views (`pair_frame=1`), and the consumer element carries `scale` (MLT's XML loader then gives it a cloned profile of its own) with twice the width: the services see the monitors' arrangement (the monitors' consumer already had a profile of its own). The timeline preview's chunks are prepared the same way, and `kdenlive_render` gives the chunk consumer a cloned profile.
- **Crop is not a service that cuts the picture where it stands.** MLT's `crop` filter added to a clip (Kdenlive's Crop effect) is the inactive kind: its `process` only leaves on the frame what it takes (`crop.left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom`, in the samples of the size it was told, `crop.original_width` and `_height`) and the cropped size in `meta.media`; the producer's own active `crop` (a chain's normalizer link, under every filter) cuts the producer's picture, and its `resize` fits what is left to the profile. In a 3D project `stereo3d_unpack` asks for the producer's picture of two views, so that crop cut the views as one picture and the rescaler, which asks the layer below for `meta.media.width` x `meta.media.height`, asked for the cropped size and stretched it back. MLT 0020: while asking, `stereo3d_unpack` sets the four values to 0 and `meta.media` to the producer's own size, then cuts each view (the amounts scaled from the size the crop was told to the view's samples) and fits the cut view to its half by its new shape as `filter_resize` does (`rint` of the shapes). With Kdenlive's default "Use Project Resolution" the amounts are those of a 2D project of that eye alone; with it off they are taken as samples of the eye's half of the frame, which is the view's own for a clip of the project's kind.
- **A frame sequence is paired in the stream's frames.** The clip monitor's `stereo3d_unpack` (frame_sequence, `eye_request`, `resource`) fetched the other frame of a pair at the next position in the profile's rate: a 50 fps sequence in a 25 fps project is decoded at frames 2p, so the partner at p+1 was frame 2p+2, the same view. MLT 0020 gives the partner producer a profile of its own at the video stream's rate (`meta.media.N.stream.frame_rate`, known when the file opens; `meta.media.frame_rate_num` only when the decoder starts) and opens it again at that rate (a producer counts its length in the rate it was opened with: opened at 25 it clamped every seek past 0.36 s to its last frame), and finds the frame's place in the stream as the avformat producer does (`position / fps * stream_fps + 0.5`). The eye is the frame's place in its pair exchanged by `invert` (H.264 D.2.26 content_interpretation_type 2; FFmpeg's `view` is the place, not the eye).
- **A clip already in the pair shape** (3840x1080 in a 1920x1080 project) keeps its `stereo3d_unpack`: the filter hands its picture on as it is, in the format and depth asked for, so its frames carry the mark and its effects act on each eye.

### Proof

The rig is the lane's scripts (a headless Stereo KWin as in [`stereo-proof-rig`](../../stereo-proof-rig/SKILL.md), Kdenlive with a generated one-track project):

- **Monitor:** one fresh session per case, `kwin_wayland --virtual --width 1920 --height 1080` with the AMD render node, `WAYLAND_DEBUG=client`, ScreenShot2 `CaptureWorkspace`. A pair is a 3840x1080 capture; inside the area where the eyes differ the left eye is red and the right eye blue, outside it 0 pixels differ; one `kde_stereo_content_v1.set_content(3)`.
- **Renders:** `kdenlive --render --render-preset NAME project.kdenlive out.EXT` with `QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen`; the marks read back with FFmpeg's `trace_headers` (H.264 `frame_packing_arrangement_*`, H.265 `fp_*`), ffprobe's stream side data, `mkvinfo` and an MP4 box walker for `st3d`; one frame per clip analysed for red left, blue right, the picture filling the frame and the white square at the same place and size in both eyes.
- **Thumbnails:** the bin's first row of the capture: red only, and the fixture's square square.
- **Effects and compositions on each eye** (`scripts/geometry-matrix.sh`, `eyes4-check.py`): each case is rendered three times with the lossless "FFmpeg FFV1" preset: the 3D project of a pair, and the same project made 2D with the left eye alone and with the right eye alone. Each eye of the 3D render must equal its 2D control: then the service acted on that eye, in that eye's coordinates, with nothing of the other eye in it. With address-coded eyes (every sample carries its eye, column and row) the equality is exact (0 differing samples in each eye, both cards' runs) for Transform moved and rotated, Position and Zoom moved, Crop by rectangle, Rotate and Shear, Obscure, Box Blur, Composite and Transform moved, Composite moved, Affine moved, Wipe and a 2D clip moved over a 3D clip. A service that scales asks a 2D producer for a smaller picture (FFmpeg's scaler) but gets the whole eye in 3D and scales it itself, so those cases use flat colour eyes (left red, right blue, a white square, the right one 40 samples further right) and check that no sample has the other eye's colour, that the square and the picture sit where the 2D control puts them within a sample, and that the clip's own disparity is scaled with it.
- **The rows a person sees on screen** (continuation 6, `monitor-case-6.sh` with a row's UI script and `ui-lib-6.sh`): a word to click is found on the capture by tesseract's word boxes (`ocr-find.py find`, the picture scaled 2x, the left eye only), a sentence is read the same way, its words grouped by line (`ocr-find.py text`; tesseract's own plain-text output garbled a panel that its word boxes read cleanly). The told sentence of a whole-frame effect: the window maximised (a double click on the title bar), the clip under the playhead selected with Plus (`k:78`), the effect's panel read; in German with `LANG=de_DE.UTF-8 LANGUAGE=de` for Kdenlive and `deu` for tesseract. The depth notice: Ctrl+Return, the preset MP4-H264/AAC (8 bits) clicked by its word, Render to File: an information box "The render goes ahead:" with the notice; a 12 bit 2D clip gets none. ## Traps

- **Symptom:** a person who chose Packed Frame as It Is in the published Kdenlive gets 3D after the default changed. **Cause:** with the default 0, choosing 0 writes nothing: `KConfigSkeletonGenericItem::writeConfig()` reverts to the default when the value equals it and no system-wide default exists (`if ((mDefault == mReference) && !cg.hasDefault(mKey)) cg.revertToDefault(...)`), so that account's file holds no choice. **Fix:** none possible for that account; with the new default every choice, Packed included, is written and kept. **How found:** a KCoreConfigSkeleton test writing 0, 1 and 2 under default 0 and default 2.
- **Symptom:** `stereo3d_unpack` on a clip's producer gives a 7680x540 frame, or a pillarboxed pair with the views squashed. **Cause:** the producer's normalizing filters (swscale, resize) are asked for the profile's size and pad the packed frame before the filter sees it; `resize` reads the frame's `meta.media.width`, `meta.media.height` and `aspect_ratio` when the frame is made. **Fix:** `in_producer`: the filter announces its output in `filter_process`. **How found:** a small C program with a loader producer, the filter first or last.
- **Symptom:** in Kdenlive the same filter still gives a half-black, squashed picture. **Cause:** Kdenlive's clips are MLT chains: their normalizers are links (`chain_normalizers.ini`), before every filter, so no filter order puts one before them. **Fix:** the filter asks the links for the producer's own picture with the frame's `distort` set, and fits each view to the profile itself. **How found:** `mlt_chain_attach_normalizers()` in `src/framework/mlt_chain.c`.
- **Symptom:** a project's clips keep their packing after loading a saved project. **Cause:** the timeline's producers come from the document (`ProjectClip::giveMasterAndGetTimelineProducer`), not from `getTimelineProducer`. **Fix:** attach in both, removing a filter marked `kdenlive:stereo3d_unpack` first.

## An export through MLT that never ends: a stall told from a slow start (ORDER 74, 2026-11-26)

Kdenlive's speech-to-text first writes the chosen audio as a WAV file through MLT's avformat consumer.
On a busy machine that export sometimes never ended: "Starting audio export" for good, a zero-byte WAV.
This part says what the fault was, how the edition's build tells a stall from a slow start, and how the proof holds on a loaded machine.
The same method serves any program that waits for an MLT consumer (`melt`, a render, the timeline preview).

### The cause: MLT's avformat consumer start (upstream issue 1333)

- **The two lines.** `consumer_start()` in `src/modules/avformat/consumer_avformat.c` of MLT 7.40.0 creates the encoding thread and only then sets the property the thread loops on: `pthread_create(thread, NULL, consumer_thread, consumer);` then `mlt_properties_set_int(properties, "running", 1);`.
  When the new thread reaches `while (mlt_properties_get_int(properties, "running") ...` first, it skips the loop and ends; the starter then sets `running`, and `consumer_is_stopped()` is false for ever with no thread behind it.
- **What each program shows.** A program that waits in its own process freezes: Kdenlive up to +stereo3d14 exported inside the editor, its main thread in `Mlt::Consumer::run()` (`mlt_events_wait_for`), every window dead. A program that polls (`melt`, the edition's `kdenlive_render speech-audio`) sleeps and polls for ever with no thread of MLT's consumer left: `melt` with one thread, `kdenlive_render` with two, its own and Qt's.
- **Upstream's repair.** [MLT pull request 1366](https://github.com/mltframework/mlt/pull/1366) for [issue 1333](https://github.com/mltframework/mlt/issues/1333), merged on 2026-11-22 after v7.42.0: four commits by Erich Seifert (`running` set before the thread starts), tydude001 (the start waits for the thread) and Brian Matherly (the handshake hardened; an event waiter no longer blocks or loses an event).
  The issue was reported from macOS at about 1 render in 250; on Linux the same fault needs only a loaded machine.
  The edition carries the four commits as MLT patches 0022 to 0025 (7.40.0-1+stereo3d13), each under its author, without the commits' additions to upstream's test files, which need the later tree.
- **Counts** (ordinary speech audio exports one after another, each arm in its own container at 0.75 CPU, side by side under one load; one-minute load 26 when the arms started, 44 when the +stereo3d14 arm was started again, 45 to 72 when they ended):

  | Kdenlive | MLT | Exports | Stalled | How each stall showed |
  |---|---|---:|---:|---|
  | +stereo3d14 (exports in the editor) | +stereo3d12 | 291 | 12 | the editor frozen in `Mlt::Consumer::run()` |
  | +stereo3d15 (its own renderer, 20 s watchdog) | +stereo3d12 | 303 | 15 | the program's own timeout; the renderer left with two threads |
  | +stereo3d15 | +stereo3d12's source without the four commits, built like the next row (control) | 72 | 7 | 6 timeouts as above, 1 "Audio export failed" at once |
  | +stereo3d15 | +stereo3d12's source with the four commits | 600 | 0 | none |

  The control row tells the commits from the build: the same toolchain and flags without them stall like the packaged library.
  With about one stall in twenty under load, 600 exports without one leave a chance below one in ten to the thirteenth that the fault is still there at that rate.
- **Search first.** The earlier round found the pull request and contained the stall in Kdenlive instead of trying it. A candidate fix from upstream is tried before any containment: here it was one development build of two libraries (`libmlt-7.so`, `libmltavformat.so`) mounted over the installed ones in the rig.

### Make the race certain

A fault that shows once in twenty runs under load and once in hundreds without is proved faster by forcing its order.
[`scripts/mlt-consumer-start/hold-after-thread-create.c`](../scripts/mlt-consumer-start/hold-after-thread-create.c) is a preloaded library that holds the caller for some milliseconds right after it created a thread of one named library, so the new thread always runs first.
With `HOLD_AFTER_THREAD_CREATE_IN=libmltavformat` and 300 ms, unrepaired MLT stalled at the first export in every run here and repaired MLT completed in every run.
A program that is right in every order is unaffected, so the same library is the regression test.
Measured on 2026-11-26 with the order forced:

| Program | MLT | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `melt` writing a WAV | +stereo3d12 | never ends: after 40 s one thread, 0 bytes, one processor tick between two looks 20 s apart |
| `melt` writing a WAV | +stereo3d13 | ends in 0.8 s with its 1,920,078-byte WAV |
| Kdenlive +stereo3d14 | +stereo3d12 | the editor frozen at its first export (its main thread's ticks 437 and 437, no audio) |
| Kdenlive +stereo3d16 | +stereo3d12's two libraries | ended at 23.9 s and said, the line `phase=5:consumer-running last_frame=-1 threads=2`, no thread of `libmltavformat` in the stack |
| Kdenlive +stereo3d16 | +stereo3d13 | ten forced exports of ten complete, 2.3 to 3.2 s each |

### What the editor follows, and what it writes when it gives up

The export runs in `kdenlive_render speech-audio ...`, started by `SpeechAudioJob` (`src/speech/speechaudiojob.cpp`, Kdenlive patches 0030 and 0031).

- **The renderer reports** one line per step on its standard output, `speech-audio PHASE FRAME`: 1 started, 2 MLT ready, 3 project loaded, 4 consumer made, 5 consumer running (FRAME is the last frame the consumer handled, from MLT's `consumer-frame-show` event), 6 done.
- **Progress** is a new phase, or a further frame in phase 5. Before phase 5 the renderer has nothing to report between two phases, and a long project loads inside one of them: there the processor time the renderer uses (`/proc/PID/stat`, fields 14 and 15) counts as progress.
  In phase 5 only frames count: a consumer that never delivers leaves the renderer polling, and polling uses a little processor time for ever.
- **After 20 seconds without progress** the export is ended and said ("Audio export timed out after 20 seconds without audio."), Process is enabled again, and the editor's log keeps one line:
  `Speech audio export timeout: renderer=alive pid=N phase=5:consumer-running last_frame=-1 threads=2 cpu_ms=340 elapsed_ms=20600 idle_ms=20010`.
- **Why 20 seconds.** The figure only bounds how long a person waits to be told of a stall; a living export does not come near it. The longest stretch without progress measured in living exports: 263 ms in 185 ordinary exports at one CPU under a load of 30, 599 ms in 333 exports sharing 0.75 CPU with four busy workers, 252 ms in a start that works 35 s before its consumer, 312 ms in a 190 s export of 90,000 frames at half a CPU. 20 s is over thirty times that, a stall is said within 20 to 24 s of the click, and 20 is the figure in the sentence already translated in the ten languages, so it stays.
- **Reading the line:**

| The line says | What it was |
|---|---|
| `phase=5 last_frame=-1`, two threads | The consumer was started and never handled a frame: MLT's start race (the stack has no thread of `libmltavformat`) |
| `phase=5 last_frame=N` | The consumer stopped advancing after frame N: read the stack for the thread that waits |
| `phase` below 5, `cpu_ms` small | No new phase and no processor time for 20 s: the renderer waits on something (a held file, a lock) before its consumer exists |
| `renderer=gone` | The renderer ended and the editor was not told: read its exit in the log line before |

A slow start never produces the line: a renderer that works keeps moving its phase, its frame or its processor time.
Five natural timeouts of +stereo3d16 on +stereo3d12's two libraries, under busy workers in the export's own container, all read the same: `renderer=alive phase=5:consumer-running last_frame=-1 threads=2`, 350 to 450 ms of processor time, said 22.6 to 23.3 s after the click; each stack held only the renderer's polling main thread and Qt's event queue, and each trace a consumer made and no frame handled: five stalls, no slow start.
With `QT_LOGGING_RULES=default.debug=true` every export also writes `Speech audio export ended: ... longest_wait_ms=N`, the longest stretch without progress, which is the figure to read before changing the 20 seconds.

### The rig: the state at the moment of the timeout, in every case

- **The renderer's trace** (a preloaded library in `kdenlive_render` only, chosen by `/proc/self/exe`): process start, `mlt_factory_consumer`, and every `consumer-frame-show` with a monotonic time, one line each.
- **The stack at the kill.** A preloaded library in the editor replaces `QProcess::kill()`: when the process being killed is the speech renderer it first runs `gdb -batch -ex "thread apply all bt" -p PID` into the case's folder, then calls the real `kill`. So every case that ends in the timeout keeps the renderer's all-thread stack of that moment, not only a soak.
  The debugger is a sibling of the renderer, which Yama's `ptrace_scope` 1 refuses; the trace library in the renderer calls `prctl(PR_SET_PTRACER, PR_SET_PTRACER_ANY)` first.
- **The rows** of the watchdog's proof: the forced race on the old editor (frozen), on the new editor with the old MLT (ended, said, the line and the stack kept) and on the repaired pair (ten of ten complete); a start that works 35 s before its consumer exists (completes; the build that watched only the bytes written ends it at 20 s, which is the control); a long clip under a CPU limit (longer than 20 s, completes); a renderer asleep before its consumer (ended at 20 s and said).
  Measured: the start that works 35 s, on +stereo3d15, was ended at 23.6 s while the renderer's processor ticks went from 919 to 1859 between the first and last look (the control); on +stereo3d16 it completed at 36.9 s. The export at half a CPU took 190.3 s with frames never more than 0.077 s apart, and completed. The renderer asleep before its consumer was ended at 24.1 s and said, its line `phase=3:project-loaded last_frame=-1 cpu_ms=290 idle_ms=20040`.

### Waits that hold on a busy machine

The proof failed on a loaded station in rows whose program was right. Each cause was a wait on the clock:

- **A wait ends on the state it reads.** Its figure only bounds a failure, lives in one helper (`wait()` of the rig's `speech_baseline.py`) and grows with the load there (four times the figure, times the load average over the processor count when that is above one). No row carries a scaling of its own.
- **A frozen program is a state, not a time.** An editor that exports in its own process has no timeout to read. It is frozen when its main thread waits in `mlt_events_wait_for` and, between two looks 30 s apart, that thread used no processor time and no audio was written. A harness limit that expires is recorded by its own name ("harness wait expired: ..."), never counted as a stall.
- **The debugger is given the time it needs.** `gdb` on an editor with 1500 threads took longer than 25 s on a loaded machine and the limit killed the row; a stack is read for a state, so its limit is minutes.
- **An overlap is made, not awaited.** The autosave rows need an autosave while the audio export runs. The instrument finds Kdenlive's autosave timer (a `QTimer::start()` wrapper that knows its interval), queues its timeout when the export has reached its consumer, and keeps the export there until the test has seen the recovery file (a file the test creates releases it). The control (the build before the fix) is read from the main thread's stack: `LockForWrite` under `QTimer::timeout` under `processEvents` under the button's click.
- **A held renderer must look alive when it is meant to.** The editor ends a renderer that neither reports nor uses processor time for 20 s, so an instrument that parks the renderer for a test works a little at each look; one that sleeps is the "held" row.
- **A helper's own limit is the same kind of clock.** The stereo row's text finder had 12 s for one OCR call; it now waits for the result, and the row's own bound covers a hang.
- **A fixed sleep before a dialog** ("wait 0.6 s, then look for OK once") became a look for the dialog inside the wait for the saved file.
- **The loaded run states its load.** Three consecutive whole runs of the rerun (53 rows each, every one as expected, two CPUs per row) ran beside a load container held to one CPU: one-minute load 22.9, 42.9 and 28.6 at their starts and 44.8, 29.1 and 26.9 at their ends, on a 12-thread machine. The load container raised the average by about 20 and took one CPU; what competed for the rows' own CPU share was the machine's other work. A load average alone does not make a race show (see the traps): say what the load was made of.

### Traps of this part

- **Symptom:** "Audio export timed out after 20 seconds without audio." in ordinary cases on a busy machine, about one export in twenty; nothing in the log says why. **Cause:** MLT's start race above; the +stereo3d15 editor contained it and kept no state. **Fix:** MLT patches 0022 to 0025; Kdenlive 0031 keeps the renderer's state in the log. **How found:** the renderer's stack at the timeout (two threads, the main one in `QThread::sleep` under the poll loop, none of `libmltavformat`), then the two lines of `consumer_start()`, then the forced order.
- **Symptom:** an export that works on its start for more than 20 s (a long project loading) is ended although it lives. **Cause:** a watchdog that counted only bytes written, or only phases. **Fix:** processor time counts before the consumer runs. **How found:** the slow-start row, red on the build that watched bytes.
- **Symptom:** a soak's "stalled" count includes exports that were only slow. **Cause:** the harness's own 8 s wait for the consumer was counted as a stall. **Fix:** a row records why it ended: the program's own timeout, the frozen state, or the harness's expired wait by name.
- **Symptom:** the soak's old-editor arm dies at its first stall with `subprocess.TimeoutExpired` for gdb. **Cause:** 25 s for `thread apply all bt` on an editor with 1506 threads: the editor that exports in its own process keeps the FFmpeg decoders of every export open (108 FFV1 decoders with 14 frame threads each after 54 exports). **Fix:** the main thread's stack for the state (`-ex "thread 1" -ex bt`), minutes for the full one. **How found:** thread names counted in the partial stack.
- **Symptom:** an editor frozen in `Mlt::Consumer::run()` is never told frozen, and the soak waits for hours with no new line in its log. **Cause:** the frozen test compared the whole process's processor time between two looks, and after 92 exports in one session the editor held 2,565 threads (FFmpeg decoders kept open), so the debugger's own look at them between two readings cost the process more than its five-tick margin. **Fix:** read the main thread's own time (`/proc/PID/task/PID/stat`, fields 14 and 15), which stays still under the debugger. **How found:** read from outside the container, without gdb, three times 30 s apart: the main thread's ticks stayed at 3033, its wait channel `futex_do_wait`, the WAV at 0 bytes.
- **Symptom:** a soak trial on unrepaired MLT waits until its harness limit, while the editor's dialog says "Audio export failed". **Cause:** most likely the start race's other order (seen once in 72 exports, on unrepaired MLT only, and not traced write by write): the consumer's thread clears `running` after the starter set it, so the renderer finds the consumer stopped at once and ends with an empty WAV, and the editor says the export failed; the harness only waited for the model's message or "timed out". **Fix:** every message the dialog can end with ends a trial, each with its own reason ("the program said the export failed"). **How found:** the screenshot the harness took when its limit expired, then the editor's stack (idle in `QDialog::exec`, not in MLT).
- **Symptom:** the stereo row's text reading fails ("jufps", "juffps" for "jumps") while the glyphs of both eyes overlap at 0.995 with no shift. **Cause:** a label of the monitor's ruler (`MonitorRuler.qml` shows its zone and marker labels while the ruler believes the pointer is on it) lay over the caption: a small white box with a dash, the same in both eyes, at the middle of the ruler whatever the seek's place. The seek clicks the ruler and then jumps the pointer out of the window's monitor in one motion, and the ruler's hover state stayed behind: the box stayed for seven minutes with the pointer on the menu bar, and moving the pointer around outside the monitor did not clear it. It came and went in earlier runs too, mostly away from the caption's letters. **Fix:** a capture counts only when the caption's band has no solid bright block (a square of 5 by 5 bright pixels; glyph strokes are 2 to 3 pixels wide); each refused look moves the pointer inside the monitor's own scene, onto the upper part of the picture away from the ruler, then out of it, and looks again. One to three such moves cleared it. **How found:** the caption cropped from both eyes and looked at, then the whole capture (the menu bar's item lit under the pointer, the box still there), then the ruler's QML; the check is true on the failing capture and false on the clean captions of an earlier run.
- **Symptom:** a run meant to collect the stall's natural timeouts gets none in 185 exports at one CPU and none in 112 at 0.75 CPU, beside a load container that raised the load average to 30. **Cause:** the race needs the renderer taken off the processor right after it created the consumer's thread; a load container held to one CPU raises the load average without taking processor time from anyone, and nothing competed for the export's own share. The soak's arms had that competition: three editors side by side on a station at load 45 to 72. **Fix:** state a load that competes for the share: busy workers inside the export's own container (two at 0.75 CPU gave one timeout in 164 exports, four gave four in 337). **How found:** the two empty runs against the soak's one in twenty.
- **Symptom:** a soak or diagnostics run ends at a random export with `ProcessLookupError: [Errno 3] No such process` from reading `/proc/PID/cmdline`. **Cause:** the harness lists the editor's children and reads each one's command line; a child that ended between the two reads raises `ProcessLookupError`, not the `FileNotFoundError` it caught, more often when the container is busy. **Fix:** catch both. **How found:** the traceback, at the twelfth export of a run with four busy workers.
- **Symptom:** a script stopped with `kill PID` leaves its container running. **Cause:** bash runs a trap only after its foreground command returns, and `docker start -a` returns when the container ends. **Fix:** stop the container by its name; the script's trap then runs and removes it. Give each run's containers a name with the run's id so that is safe.
- **Symptom:** the station's load average stays at 30 to 45 for hours with idle processors. **Cause:** a load container of an earlier run left running: 160 busy workers held to one CPU in 10 ms periods raise the load average by 20 to 30 while taking one CPU. **Fix:** the load is started and removed by the run that needs it (a trap), and every stated load says what it is made of. **How found:** the load fell from 34 to 12 within minutes of removing the container.
- **Symptom:** a cue of real speech ends seconds early, at a pause inside its own text. **Cause:** the cue's end was moved to the end of the last stretch of speech lying mostly (60 %) inside it; speech that ran on into the next cue belonged to neither, so the end fell back to the stretch before the pause. **Fix:** an edge moves only where that takes quiet away (the edge word reaches into the cue's own speech, or nothing sounds between them); clicks shorter than 80 ms no longer join two stretches. **How found:** the cue's words against the waveform of the exported WAV, then two made recordings with the answer known (the earlier source ends them 3.5 s and 1.98 s early).
- **Symptom:** a change to the speech envelope looks right on synthetic speech and worse on a recording, or the reverse. **Fix:** replay the recorded server replies of real recordings against hand-checked references for every variant before choosing (five variants here: the one chosen moved the cues within limits from 2 to 4 in English and 17 to 18 in Brazilian Portuguese, and none left them).
- **Symptom (packaging):** the link gate of MLT or Kdenlive fails in its clean root with "predecessor failed archive-qualified resolution ... undefined av_multiview_real_to_double". **Cause:** deb-multimedia's FFmpeg had moved past the round's, and the predecessor was resolved against it. **Fix:** the round's FFmpeg libraries and development packages as edition siblings on both sides of the gate, as an installed edition has them by its pin.
