# GTK 4 and GTK 3: a GtkGLArea as a declared 3D area inside an ordinary window

Written on 2026-11-07 (ORDER 137, milestone 1: GTK 4.24.1 on Wayland); milestone 3 (2026-11-25) added the X11 route without the readback, views with a size of their own, a real player and depth and colour state; milestone 4 (2026-11-26) the same area in GTK 3.24.52, at the end of this page: [GTK 3](#gtk-3).
The general rules are in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md); this page is what GTK needs on top of them.

## The route

GtkGLArea draws into a framebuffer of its own and hands GTK one texture, which GTK appends to the scene graph as a texture node.
The window's own surface carries everything else, so the area is the one place that needs a surface of its own, as the pattern says.
GTK already has the machinery: `GtkGraphicsOffload` puts a subtree on a `GdkSubsurface` (a `wl_subsurface` with a `wp_viewport`) when the subtree is one texture and nothing blocks it, and draws it normally otherwise.
So a stereo GtkGLArea is a GtkGLArea that

1. renders each view into a framebuffer of the area's size, the program's render handler unchanged (it asks `gtk_gl_area_get_current_eye()` which camera to use), and copies each view with `glBlitFramebuffer` into its half of one opaque texture twice as wide as the area (left half first);
2. appends that texture inside a subsurface node, in a clip of the area's size, with the node's rectangle twice the area's width, and declares the `GdkSubsurface` with `kde_stereo_content_v1.set_content(3)` (the protocol's version 2: a declared surface is one view wide, and `wp_viewport` destination is one view, so the area keeps its size on the screen and the source is the whole texture);
3. tells the program when it cannot do this (`stereo-shown`, `stereo-error`, one log message) and then renders the left view alone.

## The X11 route (GDK 4 on Xwayland, milestone 2)

GDK 4 has no subsurface on X11, so the area is a child X window, the pattern of [Declaring an X11 child window](../../native-linux/references/x11-video-child.md):

1. a frame window the size of one view, a child of the toplevel's X window at the area's place (`gtk_widget_compute_bounds()` into the native plus `gtk_native_get_surface_transform()`, times the scale factor), and inside it a window twice as wide that holds the two views side by side; no events selected on either, so the pointer and the keys go to the toplevel;
2. the inner window is declared with `stereo_declare_x11_child(display, toplevel, inner, STEREO_SBS_FULL, class, subclass, view_width, view_height)`, the shown size being the frame's; GDK loads `libstereo-declare.so.1` with `g_module_open` when the first stereo area asks (loaded when needed; `libgtk-4-1` depends on `libstereo-declare1 (>= 1.7.0)`, because X11 stereo areas are a designed feature) and needs `_KDE_NET_WM_STEREO_CONTENT_SUPPORTED` 8 or more;
3. the views are read back from the stereo texture (`glReadPixels`, rows turned over, red and blue swapped) and put into the inner window with `XPutImage`: one copy through the CPU, each view at the resolution it was drawn at. The readback route stays for now (it works and is measured); a GL route (an EGL surface on the inner window) is the next step and the speed-up, the picture is the same.
4. an area that cannot do it says so as on Wayland (`stereo-error`: no library, a window manager below version 8, no stereo window manager) and draws its left view alone.

Measured on Stereo KWin +46's Xwayland (client on llvmpipe): 9 of 9 checks (every sample of each eye), resize, another size, off and on, hide and show, two areas in one window; the child is listed in the toplevel's `_KDE_NET_WM_STEREO_CONTENT_CHILDREN`, declared 3, with the one view size 480x270.

What GDK needs from KWin and Mesa (both are lane 160's, nothing was changed here):
- KWin: an acknowledgement that the declared child is redirected (a property or an event) so the first frames need not be repeated (see the trap); the child cut to its window and under its siblings (the clipping draft); and the child's place with "below the parent's own surface" (`stereo_declare_x11_child_place()`, support 7) together with a transparent hole in the toplevel, so that a 2D widget drawn over the area shows over it as on Wayland. Without it a `GtkOverlay` label over an X11 stereo area is hidden (0 samples of it in either eye, measured).
- Mesa: nothing; the GLX and EGL child listing is not used (GDK lists the child itself with the library).

## The X11 route without the readback (milestone 3)

The area's own context draws the two views on the declared child window: on a graphics card no copy leaves the card.

1. The inner window gets an OpenGL surface of the display's own configuration: `eglCreateWindowSurface(gdk_display_get_egl_display(), gdk_display_get_egl_config(display, GDK_MEMORY_U8), inner, NULL)` on EGL, `glXCreateWindow` with the display's GLX configuration on GLX. The window was created with the toplevel's visual, which is that configuration's.
2. For each frame: make the child's surface current with the area's context (`eglMakeCurrent(dpy, child, child, context)`), `glDrawBuffer(GL_BACK)` (or `glDrawBuffers` on ES), bind the area's pair framebuffer for reading and framebuffer 0 for drawing, one `glBlitFramebuffer` of the whole pair, swap interval 0, swap, then make the context current as it was.
3. The first frames are repeated until KWin has taken the declaration: it sets `_KDE_NET_WM_STEREO_DEPTH_LIMITS` on a declared child it redirects (`_KDE_NET_WM_STEREO_CONTENT_SUPPORTED` 11 or more): 50 tries 20 ms apart, then stop; an older window manager gets the ten repeats of milestone 2.
4. A stereo-asking area on X11 does not export its picture as a dma-buf: nothing takes it there.
5. The way follows the renderer: OpenGL on a graphics card, the readback (milestone 2's `glReadPixels` and `XPutImage`) on a software renderer (`GL_RENDERER` names llvmpipe or softpipe), and the readback too for a display that gives the window no OpenGL surface (said in the log: "through memory"). `GDK_X11_STEREO_READBACK=1` takes the readback and `=0` OpenGL on any renderer; `G_MESSAGES_DEBUG=Gtk` prints the way taken and why.

Timed for the same work, two 1920x1080 views rendered and handed over at every frame for ten seconds in an area shown at 960x540 (`glarea-stereo-test --stereo --bench --size 960x540 --view-size 1920x1080 --report FILE`, once with `GDK_X11_STEREO_READBACK=0` and once with `=1`; the report's `bench-frames`, `bench-wall-ms` and `bench-cpu-ms` give the two columns; from the installed packages, a loaded machine, load 32 to 42 on 12 threads; the last of seven such runs):

| Client route | Way | Frames a second | Processor ms a frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mesa zink on the RTX 3060 | OpenGL | 59.2 | 1.99 |
| | readback | 35.4 | 18.03 |
| NVIDIA's own EGL | OpenGL | 59.8 | 1.00 |
| | readback | 49.6 | 15.27 |
| llvmpipe | OpenGL | 29.9 | 91.47 |
| | readback | 30.0 | 67.11 |

Judge by the processor a frame, not the frames a second: the frame clock caps the second at the display's rate, and the readback's frames moved with the machine's load (18 to 56 a second on the graphics card across the runs) while its 14 to 19 ms of processor a frame did not.
On a graphics card the OpenGL way takes an eighth to a sixteenth of the readback's processor (0.9 to 2.8 ms a frame in every run) and holds the display's rate.
On a software renderer both ways copy through memory. The readback took less processor a frame in all seven loaded runs (62 to 75 ms against 87 to 102), which is the measure the default rests on, so it is what the area takes by itself there; its frames a second went both ways with the load (from 30 % fewer than the OpenGL way to 24 % more). No run was on a quiet machine: that default is the least certain of the three, and `GDK_X11_STEREO_READBACK=0` gives the other way.
The published +stereo3d2 has the readback alone and no view size: its views are the area's size, a quarter of these samples, for about 5.5 ms a frame.

What is still owed for X11, with the exact case: **a 2D widget drawn over an X11 stereo area is hidden** (`glarea-stereo-test --stereo --overlay` on `GDK_BACKEND=x11`: 0 samples of the label in either eye; the same on Wayland shows it in both).
KWin has what it takes since +stereo3d64 (the declared child placed below its parent's own surface, version 8; the child cut to its window and under its siblings: a plain X window mapped over half the area shows whole and equal in both eyes, `--x11-sibling`).
The missing piece is GDK's: the X11 toplevel is one window whose pixels are opaque where the area is, so a child placed below shows nothing. GDK needs the area as a subsurface on X11 too (a `GdkSubsurface` implementation over the child window), so that GSK cuts the hole and decides above or below as it does on Wayland. That is a piece of its own; until then an X11 player keeps its on-screen controls out of the video (Celluloid's are a bar below it; mpv's OSD is drawn by mpv inside each view).

## Views with a size of their own

`gtk_gl_area_set_stereo_view_size(area, width, height)` (properties `stereo-view-width`, `stereo-view-height`; -1 is the area's own size).
A film's views are rendered at the size the player gives and the pair handed to the compositor is twice that width: the compositor does the one resize (the `wp_viewport` destination on Wayland, `_KDE_NET_WM_STEREO_CONTENT_ONE_VIEW_SIZE` on X11).
GTK sets `glViewport` to what one emission draws before each emission for an area that asks for stereo, so a render handler reads the size from `GL_VIEWPORT` and is right in all three states: a view of a pair, the left view alone after a failure (the area's size), a plain area.
Changing between pair and left view alone changes the size of the buffers: the area emits `resize` again.

## A real program: Celluloid (GTK 4, libadwaita, libmpv's render API)

How a GTK 4 player renders once per eye:

1. libmpv gets one parameter, `MPV_RENDER_PARAM_STEREO_VIEW` with an `mpv_render_stereo_view` (mpv-dmo +stereo3d12; value 0x53560001, outside upstream's sequence). `mpv_render_context_get_info()` with the area's shown size answers `views` (2 when the frame is the full side by side pair the edition's `video-stereo` filter hands over) and `target_w`, `target_h`: the size of one view's target, which is the shown size counted in the view's own samples. A 640x360 view shown in a 960x270 area gets a 1280x360 target: the area counted in the film's own samples, with the view sample for sample in its middle, so the pair handed over is 2560x360 and nothing was resized.
2. The player sets that size with `gtk_gl_area_set_stereo_view_size()` and `gtk_gl_area_set_stereo(area, TRUE)` from an idle callback (never inside the render signal), and in the render handler passes `view = 1` or `2` by `gtk_gl_area_get_current_eye()`, with the framebuffer GTK bound and the size of `GL_VIEWPORT`.
3. The second view repeats the frame of the first (libmpv takes no new frame for it), and mpv's on-screen display is drawn once per view at the same place: it sits at the screen plane.
4. A 2D film, a film with "Enable 3D" off, and a film whose area failed are rendered with the plain call, as before.

The 3D menu is the edition's and nothing else: Enable 3D, 3D Input Format (the stereo layer's words; the list is the layer's video packings mapped to mpv's `video-stereo-input` values, half and full), Swap left and right eyes, Save the correction into the file, Display Settings (`kcmshell6 kcm_kscreen`).
**3D Input Format is offered for every film with a picture, shown in 3D or flat** (a video track that is not a cover picture: mpv's `track-list/N/type` is `video` and `track-list/N/albumart` is false); it is how a person says what a film that declares nothing holds, and unmarked half side by side is the commonest 3D film file there is.
The rule is the edition's players' own: "Keep Input Format usable on 2D content so unmarked stereo can be identified" ([a player on the video layer](../../stereo-video/references/a-player-on-the-video-layer.md), Dragon +stereo3d11), and stereo-video's "For video that declares nothing, a menu with the input format (every packing, both eye orders), Swap Eyes, left or right only, and not 3D".
The corrections are the swap and the save: offered only while the film is shown in 3D (Daniel, 2026-10-23, on a swap for a 2D video: "of course no"). A file with sound and no picture gets none of them, and neither does the window while no film is loaded (mpv's `end-file` until the next `file-loaded`).
Never gate the input format on a guess about the picture (a shape rule, a halves test): see the trap "an unmarked film not found to be a pair" below.
The words come from the layer's Qt catalogues: a script copies each `msgstr` of context `Stereo::FileChoiceMenu` into the program's gettext catalogues, so a GTK program says the same words as the Qt ones (`tests/no-sound-wording/check.py` in the build repository holds the copy of "This film has no sound." to the layer's).

The per-file store is where the player keeps the resume position: mpv's watch-later entry.
`video-stereo-input` and `video-stereo-swap` are already first in the edition's default `watch-later-options`; the player writes the entry when a correction is made (`write-watch-later-config`), sets `reset-on-next-file` for both so the next film starts clean, and deletes the entry's correction after a save.
The save is libstereo-av's `stereo_mark_save_views()` with the corrected layout (packing and eye order); it reads back what it wrote. A refusal is told with its reason and keeps the correction.
An unmarked pair is shown flat, the whole frame, until the person chooses its packing: nothing is switched on by a guess, and nothing is read back from the picture to decide what the menu offers (milestone 3 did; its review round took it out).
When the area cannot be shown per eye the person is told once what failed and why, never "this screen": on Wayland a window drawn by the processor (the renderer's name holds `llvmpipe` or `softpipe`, GTK's own rule for its X11 way) cannot share its picture, which is said as such ("Celluloid's window is drawn by the processor, not by a graphics card, and a picture for each eye needs a graphics card"); GTK gives every other cause the same error code (`GDK_GL_ERROR_NOT_AVAILABLE`), so the sentence for those points to the log, where `stereo-error`'s exact text stays. Proof without a broken machine: `GDK_DISABLE=dmabuf` on a graphics card gives the second sentence (measured with zink over an RTX 3060: the left view alone in both eyes, the second sentence told once, GTK's reason in the log); llvmpipe gives the first.

## Depth and colour state

`gtk_gl_area_set_format(area, GdkMemoryFormat)` and `gtk_gl_area_set_color_state(area, GdkColorState *)`: GTK's own names, the same two things a `GdkTexture` has.

| Format | Framebuffer | Stereo pair to the compositor |
|---|---|---|
| `GDK_MEMORY_R8G8B8A8_PREMULTIPLIED` (default) | 8 bits | `XB24` |
| `GDK_MEMORY_ABGR2101010_PREMULTIPLIED` | 10 bits | `XB30` |
| `GDK_MEMORY_R16G16B16A16_PREMULTIPLIED` (also for 12 bits: there is no format of 12) | 16 bits | `AB48` where the driver exports one; NVIDIA's EGL and zink on NVIDIA do not, and the area says so |
| `GDK_MEMORY_R16G16B16A16_FLOAT_PREMULTIPLIED` | half float | `AB4H`, alpha made one |
| `GDK_MEMORY_R32G32B32A32_FLOAT_PREMULTIPLIED` | single float | the views in single float, the pair in half float (`AB4H`): no dma-buf format is deeper |

Allocate with `gdk_memory_format_gl_format()` (it knows the ES and the desktop forms), set the same format on the `GdkGLTextureBuilder`, and set the colour state on both the GL and the dma-buf texture builder: GSK and the subsurface take it from the texture.
The colour state is a description, not a conversion: the program writes values in that state.

The test (`scripts/colour_values.py`, `check_colour.py`, `kwin_received.py`, `check_output.py`; `glarea-stereo-test --format f16 --color-state rec2100-pq --patch R,G,B ...`):
- **Patches with a prediction.** Six colours given in linear sRGB; the calculator prints the values to write in the area's state and what the output must show. Who converts decides the model: GTK for a plain area (sRGB's own curve), KWin for a stereo area's own surface (gamma 2.2, and for PQ its tone curve), KWin on an HDR output (PQ values, as floats).
- **A ramp, counted where it is rendered and where it arrives.** 256 steps over 1/256 of the range from 0.5, read back by the program from its own framebuffer in a type that keeps the depth (ES reads a framebuffer only in its own type: `GL_UNSIGNED_INT_2_10_10_10_REV`, `GL_UNSIGNED_SHORT`, `GL_FLOAT`): 2 values at 8 bits, 5 at 10, 256 at 16, 8 in half float (11 bits of mantissa at 0.5), 256 in single float; per view in a stereo area.
- **What KWin received,** from its own `WAYLAND_DEBUG=server` log: the dma-buf's fourcc and size, `set_primaries_named`, `set_tf_named`, `set_image_description` with its intent, `set_coefficients_and_range`, `kde_stereo_content_v1.set_content(3)`.
- **What the output got.** An 8-bit screenshot shows only that the transfer function and the primaries were applied. The depth at the output needs the output's own buffer: the rig's private KWin writes the virtual output's frame as floats (`KWIN_VIRTUAL_OUTPUT_READBACK`, a patch handed back for KWin), with the output in its top and bottom 3D mode so that one buffer holds both eyes.
- **The red rows:** the model in which the compositor ignores the description (the written values shown as they are) must fail the capture; the published GTK has no `format` property.

Measured (GTK 4.24.1 with the milestone 3 patches, Stereo KWin 6.7.4 +stereo3d64, zink and NVIDIA's EGL on an RTX 3060):

| Area | KWin received | SDR output | HDR output (Rec. 2020 PQ, 1000 cd/m² peak) |
|---|---|---|---|
| stereo, 8 bits, sRGB | `XB24` 960x270, sRGB primaries, gamma 2.2 | every sample what the program drew, both eyes | |
| stereo, 10 bits, sRGB | `XB30`, the same description | patches exact, 5 ramp values a view | |
| stereo, half float, Rec. 2100 PQ | `AB4H` 960x270, `bt2020`, `st2084_pq`, intent perceptual, identity coefficients and full range, no luminances | KWin's tone curve within one level (white 186), both eyes | each eye's own buffer `AB4H` as floats, each patch within 0.0004 of the prediction, 8 ramp values in each eye |
| stereo, single float | views in single float (256 ramp values), pair `AB4H` | patches exact | |
| stereo, 16 bits | no dma-buf from these drivers: the area says so, left view alone | | |
| stereo, hybrid log-gamma | not offloaded: KWin lists no HLG transfer function | left view alone, colours right (GTK converts) | |
| on X11, a colour state other than sRGB | the area says that an X window has no description | left view alone | |

The private KWin for the HDR column is the staged source with one patch (`KWIN_VIRTUAL_OUTPUT_HDR=1000`: a virtual output with Rec. 2020 PQ and that peak, drawn into a half float buffer; `KWIN_VIRTUAL_OUTPUT_READBACK=DIR`: the frame of each eye written as floats when a file `request` appears). Put the output into a 3D mode (`kscreen-doctor output.1.mode.3` after `KWIN_VIRTUAL_STEREO_MODES=1`) so that its layer has a buffer for each eye, and let the area render every frame (`--bench`): a still picture gives the output no new frame to write.
A screenshot of an HDR output is KWin's conversion to sRGB: it shows the SDR numbers and proves nothing about the output's own values.

## Recipe

- Check the source first: `GtkGLArea` renders in `gtk_gl_area_snapshot` (gtk/gtkglarea.c); `GskOffload` (gsk/gskoffload.c, `find_texture_to_attach`) decides what can be attached; `gdk_wayland_subsurface_attach` (gdk/wayland/gdksubsurface-wayland.c) does the Wayland side; `GDK_DEBUG=offload` prints each refusal with its reason.
- Add the protocol XML as a private protocol of GDK (`gdk/wayland/protocol/kde-stereo-content-v1.xml`, meson `'stability': 'private'`), bind `kde_stereo_content_manager_v1` from version 2, and declare in a new `GdkSubsurface` vfunc `set_stereo`.
- In GSK's offload, for a stereo subsurface take the whole texture as the source rectangle (a clip would otherwise crop it to the area's half).
- The rig: a headless Stereo KWin (the shared image), the program under test run in the same container against the GTK build's library directory (`LD_LIBRARY_PATH`), `GDK_DEBUG=offload` and `WAYLAND_DEBUG=1` on the client, ScreenShot2 for the capture. A stereo window's capture is twice the output's width (2560 for a 1280 output): that alone tells that KWin took the declaration.
- The test program draws with scissored clears only (a red or blue field, the tag LEFT or RIGHT in 5x7 glyphs, a frame number, a white square and a one pixel white frame), looks the stereo API up with `dlsym` and `g_object_class_find_property`, so one binary is the test on the patched GTK and the control on Debian's. A checker redraws the expected picture in Python and compares every sample of each eye.
- The cases, each in a fresh KWin session: stereo; the swapped-eye program (must fail the LEFT/RIGHT check); a resize while shown; stereo switched off and on; hidden and shown; the failure cases (a widget opacity below 1; the protocol removed with `GDK_WAYLAND_DISABLE=kde_stereo_content_manager_v1`; a context that cannot export a dma-buf); the control (stereo off against Debian's GTK).

## The scripts

- `scripts/glarea-stereo-test.c`: the test program. `cc glarea-stereo-test.c $(pkg-config --cflags --libs gtk4 epoxy) -ldl`, then run it with `--stereo` (and `--swap`, `--opacity 0.99`, `--grow-after 3`, `--toggle-after 4`, `--hide-after 3`, `--overlay`, `--offload`, `--frame N`, `--size WxH`, `--report FILE`, `--quit-after SECS`). It writes the property values, the renderer string and the eyes its render handler saw to the report file. The same binary runs on a GTK without the API (it looks the property and the getter up at run time): that is the control.
- Milestone 3 added to the test program: `--view-size WxH` (the views' own size), `--bench` (render at every frame; the report gains frames, wall and processor time), `--x11-sibling` (a plain green X window over half the area), `--scrolled` (the area half scrolled out), `--format u8|u10|u16|f16|f32`, `--color-state srgb|srgb-linear|rec2100-pq|rec2100-linear|rec2100-hlg`, `--patch R,G,B` (several: patches with these written values, an eye tag, and a ramp it reads back per view). It ends with its report on SIGTERM.
- `scripts/colour_values.py STATE [gtk|kwin|kwin-hdr [PEAK]]`: the patch values to write in a colour state and what the output must show, by who converts (GTK, KWin on an SDR output with its tone curve, KWin on an HDR output).
- `scripts/check_colour.py CAPTURE.png OUTPUT_WIDTH PATCHES.txt [--expect stereo] [--raw]`: finds the area in each eye of a capture and compares each patch; `--raw` is the red model (the written values shown as they are).
- `scripts/kwin_received.py KWIN.log`: from KWin's `WAYLAND_DEBUG=server` log, the colour, format and stereo requests it received, and the same in words (fourcc, named primaries and transfer function, intent, range).
- `scripts/check_output.py READBACK_DIR PATCHES.txt [--eyes top-bottom]`: reads the frame a virtual HDR output got (the private KWin's `KWIN_VIRTUAL_OUTPUT_READBACK`) and checks each eye's tag, patches and ramp in the output's own encoding.
- `scripts/dump_menu.py`: a GTK program's exported menu bar with labels, actions and `hidden-when`, read with `org.gtk.Menus.Start` (GDBusMenuModel gives nothing without a running main loop); the program needs its menu bar exported (Celluloid: the setting `csd-enable` off).
- `scripts/dmabuf-export-probe.c`: exports a GL texture as GTK does and says whether each plane's descriptor is open (`cc dmabuf-export-probe.c -lEGL -lGLESv2`); run it with `__EGL_VENDOR_LIBRARY_FILENAMES` naming Mesa's vendor file first and NVIDIA's second, `MESA_LOADER_DRIVER_OVERRIDE=zink` and `VK_DRIVER_FILES` for NVIDIA's Vulkan driver.
- `scripts/check_eyes.py CAPTURE.png [--size WxH] [--expect stereo|left|mono] [--overlay] [--tol N] [--swap]`: redraws the expected pictures and checks the capture: both eyes in one capture (twice the output's width), red LEFT in the left half and blue RIGHT in the right half with no red in the right and no blue in the left, the area at its own size and place in both halves, each eye's every sample equal to what the program drew, and everything outside the area identical in both eyes. `--expect left` and `--expect mono` check a 2D capture (the area that could not be shown per eye, an area without stereo). It exits 1 when a check fails, so the negative controls are runs that must exit 1.

## Traps

- **Both eyes shown squeezed in the area's place when GTK does not offload.** Symptom: the area shows two small pictures side by side. Cause: GSK draws the subsurface node's child when it cannot attach it, and the child was the double-width texture scaled to the area. Fix: the child is a clip of the area's size over a texture node twice as wide, so the fallback is the left view at its own size, and GSK's offload takes the whole texture for a stereo subsurface instead of the clip's part. Found by reading `gsk_gpu_node_processor_add_subsurface_node` and `find_texture_to_attach`.
- **No dma-buf, no subsurface.** Symptom: `🗙 Texture type not supported or export failed` in `GDK_DEBUG=offload`. Cause: GTK attaches only dma-buf textures, and llvmpipe cannot export one. Fix: none in GTK; prove the offload on a GPU (the RTX 3060 through CDI), and let the CPU-only run prove the honest failure. The area says it.
- **zink on NVIDIA ends the connection (Debian's GTK too).** Symptom: `Error 9 (Bad file descriptor) dispatching to Wayland display` right after `zwp_linux_dmabuf_v1.create_params`, in 4 of 6 runs of the stereo area and in 2 of 2 runs of a plain `GtkGraphicsOffload` around a `GtkGLArea` on Debian's gtk4 4.24.1. Cause: with Mesa's zink on NVIDIA's proprietary Vulkan driver `eglExportDMABUFImageMESA` returns success and writes no file descriptor (the modifier is `DRM_FORMAT_MOD_INVALID`), and GTK's `fds[]` was uninitialised, so it sent random integers. Fix in GTK: the descriptors start at -1 and every plane's must be open, otherwise the export has failed and GTK draws the texture itself (6 of 6 runs then end in the honest failure). Mesa's side is fixed in the edition's Mesa 26.1.6-1+stereo3d18 (0030: zink exports with a modifier NVIDIA's driver can render with; 0031: a failed export returns EGL_BAD_MATCH): with it the stereo area shows on zink over the RTX 3060 (dma-buf XB24:0x300000000606014, 9 of 9 checks), and the GTK fix stays for any driver that exports no open descriptor. Found by `GDK_DEBUG=dmabuf` (the crashing runs printed `Exported GL texture to dmabuf`), then `scripts/dmabuf-export-probe.c`, which presets the descriptors to -1 and showed -1 kept. NVIDIA's own EGL exports a valid dma-buf and works.
- **A blit ignores the colour mask and obeys the scissor.** ES 3.2 section 16.2.1: only the pixel ownership test, the scissor test and sRGB conversion affect a blit. A program that leaves `GL_SCISSOR_TEST` on (the test program does) would clip the copy: disable it around the blit.
- **Alpha.** The picture handed over is opaque (`GL_RGB8`, `B8G8R8X8`): GTK refuses a non-opaque texture on a subsurface below the window, and a stereo picture has nothing to blend with.

## GTK 3

Milestone 4 (queue row 137, 2026-11-26): the same stereo area in GTK 3.24.52, Debian's gtk+3.0 3.24.52-3 with the edition's patches (+stereo3d1), so GTK 3 programs that draw with OpenGL can be shown per eye; GIMP 3.2's cairo canvas is the next milestone ([plans](gtk-glarea-plans.md)).

### The route

GTK 3 has no scene graph and no offload: it draws a whole window into one 8 bit buffer, cairo first, and a GtkGLArea's framebuffer is copied into it (`gdk_cairo_draw_from_gl()`, gtk/gtkglarea.c). So a stereo area gives the compositor its picture itself, on a surface of its own, from its draw handler:

1. each view is rendered into the area's own framebuffer at the view's size (the program's render handler unchanged, `gtk_gl_area_get_current_eye()` says which camera), and copied with `glBlitFramebuffer` into its half of one pair, left first;
2. **Wayland:** the pair is a texture exported as a dma-buf from the area's own context (`EGL_MESA_image_dma_buf_export`, the opaque form of its fourcc where the compositor lists it: XB24, XB30, XB48, XB4H), attached to a `wl_subsurface` placed **below** the window's surface (`wl_subsurface_place_below`), sized to one view with `wp_viewport.set_destination` (the declaration's version 2: a declared surface is one view), its colour described with `wp_color_management_v1` on its own `wl_surface`, and declared with libstereo-declare's `stereo_declare_wayland_format(surface, STEREO_SBS_FULL)`. The subsurface stays synchronized, so what is committed on it shows with the window's frame GDK is painting;
3. **X11:** the pair is drawn on a child X window of the toplevel twice a view wide, declared with `stereo_declare_x11_child()` and placed with `stereo_declare_x11_child_place()` below the window's own surface when the window has an alpha channel: with the area's own GLX context (`glXMakeContextCurrent` on the child, `glDrawBuffer(GL_BACK)`, one blit, swap, the context's drawables restored) on a graphics card, through memory (`glReadPixels`, `XPutImage`) on a software renderer; `GDK_X11_STEREO_READBACK=0` or `=1` chooses, as in GTK 4;
4. the window keeps a **hole** where the area lies: the area clears its rectangle with `CAIRO_OPERATOR_CLEAR`, and GtkWindow subtracts it from the opaque region it gives the compositor (`_gtk_window_set_stereo_hole()`), so the window's own widgets drawn over the area (a `GtkOverlay` label) stay over it, in both eyes;
5. an area that cannot do this says why (`stereo-error`, one log line) and renders its left view alone into the window, until stereo is asked again. Every failure that can be known before the views are rendered is found when the pair is prepared (the dma-buf export, a colour state the compositor cannot be told, a depth or a colour state an X window cannot hold), so such an area never renders a right view.

The Wayland globals (`wl_subcompositor`, `wp_viewporter`, `zwp_linux_dmabuf_v1` version 3 for its format and modifier list, `wp_color_manager_v1`) and libstereo-declare's own objects are bound on an event queue of the area's own (`wl_display_create_queue`, a proxy wrapper for the registry), so the area can wait for the compositor's answers (a buffer created, an image description ready) inside the draw without dispatching GDK's events. GDK 3 already lets a program add its own surfaces (`gdk_wayland_window_add_frame_callback_surface`, since 3.24.25); nothing in GDK's Wayland backend changes.

### The API: GTK 4's names

`gtk_gl_area_set_stereo()` (property `stereo`), `stereo-shown`, `stereo-error` (a `GError`), `gtk_gl_area_get_current_eye()` with `GtkGLAreaEye` (NONE, LEFT, RIGHT), `gtk_gl_area_set_stereo_view_size()` (`stereo-view-width`, `stereo-view-height`; -1, or any value up to 0, is the area's own size), `gtk_gl_area_set_format()` and `gtk_gl_area_set_color_state()` (`format`, `color-state`).
GDK 3 had no `GdkMemoryFormat` or `GdkColorState`: the patch adds them under GTK 4's names and values, the five formats an area renders in (`R8G8B8A8_PREMULTIPLIED` 2, `R16G16B16A16_PREMULTIPLIED` 10, `R16G16B16A16_FLOAT_PREMULTIPLIED` 13, `R32G32B32A32_FLOAT_PREMULTIPLIED` 16, `ABGR2101010_PREMULTIPLIED` 68), the four colour state getters (sRGB, linear sRGB, Rec. 2100 PQ, Rec. 2100 linear) and `GdkCicpParams` for any other, so a program says the same in both toolkits.

### Depth and colour state in GDK 3

GTK 3 converts no colours: a colour state is handed on, never applied. So the depth and the colour state reach the compositor only on the area's own surface, which a stereo area has; a plain area with another format or colour state is drawn into the 8 bit window with its values as written, and GTK says so once in its log.
The colour state is ITU-T H.273 code points, and the Wayland side maps each to the protocol's names (color-management-v1.xml); a code point the protocol has no name for, or a name the compositor does not list, is said:

| H.273 colour primaries | `wp_color_manager_v1` primaries | H.273 transfer characteristics | transfer function |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (BT.709, sRGB) | srgb | 1, 6, 14, 15 (BT.709, BT.601, BT.2020) | bt1886 (the display curve those assume) |
| 4 (BT.470 M) | pal_m | 4 (BT.470 M) | gamma22 |
| 5 (BT.470 B, G; BT.601 625) | pal | 5 (BT.470 B, G) | gamma28 |
| 6, 7 (SMPTE 170M, 240M) | ntsc | 7 (SMPTE 240M) | st240 |
| 8 (illuminant C) | generic_film | 8 (linear) | ext_linear |
| 9 (BT.2020, BT.2100) | bt2020 | 9, 10 (logarithmic) | log_100, log_316 |
| 10 (SMPTE ST 428-1) | cie1931_xyz | 11 (IEC 61966-2-4) | xvycc |
| 11 (SMPTE RP 431-2) | dci_p3 | 13 (sRGB) | srgb, or gamma22 where the compositor does not list srgb (KWin does not) |
| 12 (SMPTE EG 432-1) | display_p3 | 16 (PQ), 17 (ST 428-1), 18 (HLG) | st2084_pq, st428, hlg |
| 22 (EBU 3213) | none: said | 12 (BT.1361) | none: said |

| Format | Views rendered in | Pair to the compositor |
|---|---|---|
| 8 bit | `GL_RGBA8` (`GL_RGB8` without alpha, as before) | XB24 |
| 10 bit | `GL_RGB10_A2` | XB30 |
| 16 bit (also 12) | `GL_RGBA16` | XB48 where the driver exports one; NVIDIA's EGL and zink on NVIDIA do not, and the area says so |
| half float | `GL_RGBA16F` | XB4H |
| float | `GL_RGBA32F` | half float, XB4H: no dma-buf format is deeper |

On X11 a colour state other than sRGB, or a format deeper than the window's visual, is said and the left view shown alone: an X window has no colour description, and its visual holds 8 bits a channel.

Where each depth stops, measured with the ramp of 256 steps over 1/256 of the range from 0.5 (counted where it is rendered, then in each eye's buffer at a virtual HDR output, Rec. 2020 PQ at 1000 cd/m², through milestone 3's private KWin):

| Area format | Ramp values rendered | Pair | At the HDR output | Where the chain stops short of 16 bits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 bit | 5 | XB30 | 5 | nowhere before the output: 10 bits arrive |
| half float | 8 | XB4H | 8 | the format itself (11 bits of mantissa at 0.5) |
| float | 256 | XB4H | 8 | the pair: no dma-buf format is deeper than half float, and the output's buffer is half float too |
| 16 bit integer | 256 | none on NVIDIA | | the export: NVIDIA's EGL and zink on NVIDIA export no 16 bit dma-buf, and the area says so |
| 12 bit | (in 16 bit or float) | | | as for 16 bit integer or float: no 12 bit format exists in GL or dma-buf |

### The proof

`m4/rerun.sh` of the lane (the edition's private record) runs each case in a fresh headless Stereo KWin from installed packages: `gtk137-m4rig:pub` (the published round with Debian's gtk+3.0) and `gtk137-m4rig:new` (the same with the staged gtk+3.0 installed by apt). The test program is `scripts/glarea-stereo-test3.c`, the GTK 4 program's twin (the same pictures, options and report, plus `--headerbar`); it looks the stereo API up at run time, so one binary is the test on the edition's GTK 3 and the control on Debian's. The judges are the GTK 4 page's (`check_eyes.py`, `check_colour.py`, `kwin_received.py`, `check_output.py`) and three of milestone 4: `check_two.py` (two areas in one window), `check_shift.py` (a scene drawn with a camera for each eye: the right view is the left one moved sideways, the rest identical) and `check_lit.py` (whether a window's own widgets reached the output).
A desktop without stereo is a compositor without the declaration in the same container: weston's headless backend for Wayland, Xvfb for X11.
GTK's own suite is run the way Debian's build runs it (X11 under Xvfb through `debian/tests/run-with-display`, a session bus, `LIBGL_ALWAYS_SOFTWARE=1`, `--timeout-multiplier 4`, Debian's fuzzy and ignored reftests), before and after the patches; `testsuite/gtk/glarea.c` is new.

### Traps

- **The picture reaches the compositor upside down (Wayland only).** Symptom: both eyes right and at the right place, but 8 % of the samples wrong: the tag LEFT mirrored at the bottom of the area. Cause: a dma-buf's first row is the top of the picture, OpenGL's first row the bottom, and the pair was copied in OpenGL's order. Fix: on Wayland the copy into the pair turns each view over (the destination rows of `glBlitFramebuffer` swapped); X11 keeps OpenGL's order, its blit and its readback turn it themselves. Found by looking at the first capture's area.
- **A widget over an X11 stereo area is hidden although the child is placed below the window.** Symptom: `_KDE_NET_WM_STEREO_CONTENT_CHILD_PLACE` ends in 1, the area shows, the label drawn over it does not (0 yellow samples), with a window that has an alpha channel. Cause: GTK 4's X11 route puts the declared child in an undeclared frame window one view wide; only the declared child is redirected, so the frame is part of the toplevel's own picture and covers the window's pixels there, label included. Fix: below the window there is no frame; the child is a child of the toplevel itself. Found by asking which X window was in the toplevel's picture over the area (`x11_tree.py`), after the plain area's window showed the label (2209 samples) and the Wayland area too.
- **A window without an alpha channel hides what lies below it.** The hole needs a window whose visual has alpha: on X11 GTK 3 gives one to a window with client-side decorations (a header bar) on a compositing window manager. A window without one keeps the child above it, and GTK says in its log that a widget drawn over the area is hidden by it.
- **The window would hide the subsurface below it.** GtkWindow tells the compositor its content is opaque (`update_opaque_region()`); a compositor draws an opaque region without blending, so the hole would show black. Fix: the window subtracts every stereo area's rectangle from its opaque region.
- **GTK 3's X11 window on zink shows nothing but its GL area (Debian's GTK 3 too).** Symptom: under Xwayland with Mesa's zink on NVIDIA's Vulkan driver, the capture of a window with a plain GtkGLArea is black but for the area (lit share 0.127 against 0.287 on NVIDIA's own GLX). Not the stereo patch: Debian's 3.24.52-3 shows the same. The stereo rows on X11 that look at the window's widgets run on NVIDIA's GLX and llvmpipe; the fault is in the lane's unfiled failures.
- **A dma-buf the compositor refuses ends the connection.** `zwp_linux_buffer_params_v1.create_immed` with a format or modifier the compositor does not take is a protocol error; use `create` and wait for `created` or `failed` on the area's own queue, and choose the fourcc from the compositor's `modifier` events (version 3) first.
- **A view rendered that can never be shown.** Symptom: on llvmpipe the area said why and showed its left view, but its render handler had drawn a right view once (`eyes-seen: left=2 right=1`, a row of the first whole run). Cause: the dma-buf export was tried when the pair was handed over, after both views were rendered. Fix: the export, and every other failure known in advance, is tried when the pair is prepared, before the first view (then `left=1 right=0`). Found by the row that asked the right view never to be rendered.
- **GTK's notify test sets 0 on every integer property.** A setter that turns 0 into -1 changes nothing and emits no notify, which the test counts as a missing notify (`gtk:notify` aborted). Keep the value as given and read anything up to 0 as "the area's own size".
- **GTK's defaultvalue test wants a boxed property's default to be NULL.** `color-state` defaults to sRGB's state; it is listed with GTK 3's other boxed defaults (GtkColorButton's `rgba`).
- **A test picture with snow is never the same twice.** `videotestsrc pattern=smpte` has a block of random noise; compare GTK builds with `pattern=colors`.
- **gtk-update-icon-cache is not a gtk+3.0 package in Debian testing** (it comes from gtk4); Debian's own binaries of 3.24.52-3 for the link gate and the file comparison are the other ten.
